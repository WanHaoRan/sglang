# Analogous systems outside LLM KV caching: keep-alive, pre-warming, user-driven prefetching, priced memory, predictive tiering

Legend used throughout: **(a)** forecasts (a client-supplied idle-gap distribution per tool-call key, conditioned on idle time); **(b)** fetch (start the read once P[request arrives before the read would complete] >= q = λL/(1+λL), a newsvendor critical ratio); **(c)** retain/evict (keep while hazard x read time >= q; evict by a hazard-rate index that reduces to Belady's MIN or LRU); **(d)** price (a Lagrangian price λ on host memory, learned by multiplicative dual ascent on demand minus capacity). "PRIOR-ART FLAG" marks papers a reviewer could cite against either (i) setting a pre-warm/keep-alive time from a distribution through a cost ratio, or (ii) pricing memory with a learned shadow price.

## Q0. Ranked list: which verified papers are closest to the design (closest first)?

### Takeaway
The closest prior art is a UAI 2023 paper (Narayana et al.) that derives serverless keep-alive and pre-warm windows from the hazard rate of the next arrival and a cost ratio (holding cost per unit time vs cold-start cost). It is essentially the design's retain rule with an exogenous, fixed holding cost, no capacity constraint, unit-size objects and instantaneous pre-warming. Next come Azure's hybrid-histogram policy (pre-warm and keep-alive percentiles chosen by tuning) and a 2026 preprint (GearUp) that picks keep-alive by minimizing D·C_keep + C_cold·P(τ > D). No verified system combines a read whose duration grows with state size, a size- and price-dependent stall-risk threshold, and a capacity price learned online. The nearest "learned price" precedents are IMP's feedback-adjusted conversion rates for energy and data budgets (mobile prefetching, 2012) and Dehghan et al.'s dual-gradient price on cache capacity (caching theory, 2016).

### Cited Findings

#### Part A: on arXiv (ranked; number = global rank across Parts A and B)

**[1] arXiv:2309.03521, "Keep-Alive Caching for the Hawkes process"**, Narayana et al., 2023, UAI 2023 (PMLR 216:1499–1509). https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03521
- *Mechanism.* The paper models keep-alive caching for serverless computing where "there is not a fixed size cache to be filled, but rather there is a cost associated with the time an item stays in the cache," trading that cost against the cost of misses ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03521)). Costs are c_p, the provider's cost per unit time of keeping the application image warm, and c_cs, the cold-start cost, "primarily the latency cost experienced by the user" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03521)). Theorem 2: the boundaries of the optimal keep-alive windows lie at 0, ∞, or where c_p − c_cs·λ(x|H) = 0 changes sign, with λ the hazard rate of the next arrival given the history. Decreasing hazard gives a single keep-alive window, and for Hawkes processes that window starts immediately. Increasing hazard gives a pre-warm time and then keep-alive forever. A single-peaked hazard gives both, "the form used by Shahrad et al. [2020]" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03521)). On Azure traces, applying the optimal policy to the 25% of applications best fit by a Hawkes process beats fixed TTLs, and a history-independent optimized TTL comes close to optimal ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03521)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a), (b) and (c). Shared: the conditional (history-dependent) arrival distribution, a hazard-rate threshold set by a cost ratio, and pre-warm and keep-alive both derived from one cost model. Differences: c_p is exogenous and fixed, with no capacity constraint and no learned price, i.e., no (d). Objects have a fixed size, so there is no size-dependent q. Pre-warming is instantaneous: a cold start occurs only if the arrival precedes τ_pw ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03521)). So there is no read duration, no newsvendor over the read window, and no memory held by in-flight reads. Arrival models are fitted per application (Poisson/Hawkes), not supplied per tool-call key by the client. There is no eviction order under contention and no preemption.
- **PRIOR-ART FLAG (i), strongest found.** It sets pre-warm and keep-alive times from a distribution through a cost ratio. The design should cite it and position its retain rule as this condition with an endogenous price, size-dependent costs and a finite, size-proportional read (see Inferences).

**[2] arXiv:2003.03423, "Serverless in the Wild: Characterizing and Optimizing the Serverless Workload at a Large Cloud Provider"**, Shahrad et al., 2020, USENIX ATC 2020 ([USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc20/presentation/shahrad)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03423
- *Mechanism.* The hybrid histogram policy keeps a per-application histogram of idle times (ITs) in 1-minute bins over a configurable range, e.g. 4 hours ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)). The head of the IT distribution sets the pre-warming window and the tail sets the keep-alive window, "by default to the 5th- and 99th-percentiles," with a 10% margin (pre-warm window shortened by 10%, keep-alive lengthened by 10%) ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)). If the histogram is not representative (CV of bin counts below a threshold, default CV = 2), the policy reverts to a fixed keep-alive equal to the histogram range. For applications with many out-of-bound ITs, it uses ARIMA to predict the next IT and pre-warms just before it ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)). The cutoffs come from "the sensitivity study that we used to determine suitable cutoff values." Hybrid[5,99] keeps the cold-start rate while cutting wasted memory time by 15% vs Hybrid[0,100], and policies are compared on cold-start vs wasted-memory Pareto curves ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)). Baseline: AWS and Azure keep functions loaded 10 and 20 minutes after execution ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a), (b) and (c). Shared: a per-entity idle-time distribution that sets both when to re-load and how long to keep. Differences: the percentiles are fixed by tuning and are the same for every application, regardless of memory footprint or current memory pressure. This is the design's "fixed quantile, one q for every read" special case (design §Discussion). The distribution is learned per application from its own arrivals and is not conditioned on the cause of the idle period. Load duration does not enter the window computation, and there is no price.
- Not a cost-ratio derivation. However, [1] shows that this (pre-warm, keep-alive) form is optimal for single-peaked hazards.

**[3] arXiv:2610.38196, "GearUp: DAG-aware Acceleration for Serverless Inference Serving via Slack-guided Pre-loading"**, Qi et al., 2026 (v1 28 Oct 2026, v2 1 Nov 2026), preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38196
- *Mechanism.* GearUp profiles the loading stages of each ML node (libraries, weights, CUDA kernels) and schedules them inside the slack that the DAG exposes, hiding initialization behind upstream execution ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38196)). Keep-alive is "a probabilistic cost optimization problem": D* = argmin_{D≥0} [D·C_keep + C_cold·P(τ > D)]. C_keep is a unit cost per second of keeping an idle instance, C_cold is the cold-start penalty and τ is the time to the next invocation. The retention term "charges the full timeout D, approximating the idle-memory cost" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38196)). P(τ > D) blends a bucketed histogram with a step-function short-term predictor via an adaptive weight w, and is conditioned on the runtime state of parent DAG nodes. A global limit is enforced by evicting the oldest idle containers ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38196)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a) and (c), and partly (b). Shared: keep-alive chosen by minimizing holding cost plus expected cold-start cost under an estimated, conditioned distribution. Differences: C_keep is a fixed constant rather than a price learned from capacity. The objective charges D even when the request arrives earlier, whereas the design charges memory-time actually held. Pre-loading is timed by deterministic DAG slack, not by an arrival-probability threshold. Contention is resolved by age-based eviction, not a value index.
- **PRIOR-ART FLAG (i).** It sets keep-alive from a distribution through a cost trade-off. v1 appeared on 28 Oct 2026, about three weeks before this survey.

**[9] arXiv:2603.03091, "Custom Keep-Alive Cache Policies"**, Narayana et al., 2026, EAI GameNets 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03091
- *Mechanism.* This is market design for keep-alive caching. Customers bid their cost of a cache miss (cold start). The allocation policy uses online learning from a mixture of fixed allocation experts and is asymptotically efficient and monotone in the bid. Customers are charged either by Myerson's theory or their externality ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03091)). With customer-reported costs, "an online learning algorithm can automatically customize the length of the keep-alive window" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03091)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (c) and (d). Shared: per-tenant cost of a miss priced into retention. Differences: the price is a payment that elicits truthful miss costs (mechanism design), not an internal shadow price on a capacity constraint. Holding cost is per unit time, so there is no fixed capacity. Experts are fixed keep-alive lengths, with no forecasts or pre-warming.
- Partial **PRIOR-ART FLAG (ii)**: it prices keep-alive memory, but not with a learned shadow price.

**[12] arXiv:2212.13882, "QoS-Aware Resource Management for Multi-phase Serverless Workflows with Aquatope"**, Zhou et al., 2022 (arXiv), ASPLOS 2023 as "AQUATOPE: QoS-and-Uncertainty-Aware Resource Management for Multi-stage Serverless Workflows" ([DOI 10.1145/3567955.3567960](https://doi.org/10.1145/3567955.3567960)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13882
- *Mechanism.* Aquatope "uses a set of scalable and validated Bayesian models to create pre-warmed containers ahead of function invocations," and allocates per-function resources to meet end-to-end QoS while minimizing cost ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13882)). According to a search-result summary of the paper, a hybrid Bayesian neural network sizes a dynamic pre-warmed container pool and Bayesian optimization picks per-stage configurations ([search summary of arXiv/ar5iv text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.13882)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a) and (b). Shared: uncertainty-aware forecasts drive pre-warming. Differences: it sizes a pool for aggregate invocations rather than timing per-session reads. The abstract gives no cost-ratio quantile, no size-dependent risk and no memory price.

**[13] arXiv:2508.07640, "Taming Cold Starts: Proactive Serverless Scheduling with Model Predictive Control"**, Nguyen et al., 2025, MASCOTS 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07640
- *Mechanism.* An MPC controller uses Fourier-based forecasts of request rates. At each control step it jointly decides prewarming, reclamation and request dispatch as a constrained optimization ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07640)). It reports up to 85% lower tail latency and 34% less resource usage on OpenWhisk ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07640)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a), (b) and (c). Shared: pre-warm and reclaim from forecasts via an explicit objective, not fixed percentiles. Differences: forecasts are aggregate rates (point forecasts), not per-session idle-time distributions. There is no learned capacity price and no size-proportional load time.

**[14] arXiv:2602.23935, "Green or Fast? Learning to Balance Cold Starts and Idle Carbon in Serverless Computing"**, Sun et al., 2026, preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23935
- *Mechanism.* LACE-RL casts pod retention as a sequential decision problem. Deep RL tunes keep-alive durations, "jointly modeling cold-start probability, function-specific latency costs, and real-time carbon intensity." On the Huawei trace it cuts cold starts by 51.69% and idle keep-alive carbon by 77.08% vs Huawei's static policy ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23935)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (c) and (d). Shared: idle retention charged at a time-varying price (grid carbon intensity) against a per-function latency cost. Differences: the price is exogenous (the grid), not a shadow price of a capacity limit. The policy is a learned RL policy rather than a closed-form threshold.

**[15] arXiv:2607.23933, "SpecBox: Speculative Sandbox Scheduling for Efficient LLM Agent Serving"**, Zhang et al., 2026, preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23933
- *Mechanism.* SpecBox pre-warms the tool sandboxes of LLM agents. It detects pending tool calls mid-generation by keyword matching and streaming semantic embeddings, and across steps it prefetches probabilistically on a sandbox dependency graph ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23933)). The keyword router uses a threshold γ on matched keywords. Cross-step prefetching uses a first-order Markov model with "thresholded, budgeted prefetching," and the threshold "balances early activation with resource waste" with the configuration chosen empirically ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23933)). It reports up to 2.9x lower P99 end-to-end latency vs on-demand sandboxes ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23933)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a) and (b), in the same agentic workload. This is the mirror image: it warms the tool side before the tool runs, whereas the design stages the LLM-side state before the session returns. Differences: thresholds and budgets are tuned, not derived. Forecasts are tool-identity predictions, not idle-time distributions.

**[16] arXiv:2403.17574, "SPES: Towards Optimizing Performance-Resource Trade-Off for Serverless Functions"**, Lee et al., 2024, ICDE 2024 ([DOI 10.1109/ICDE60146.2024.00020](https://doi.org/10.1109/icde60146.2024.00020)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17574
- *Mechanism.* SPES categorizes functions by invocation pattern and pre-loads or unloads instances with category-specific strategies "based on accurate invocation prediction." It reports 49.77% lower 75th-percentile cold-start rate and 56.43% less wasted memory time than the state of the art ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17574)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a), (b) and (c). It is a refinement of [2] with per-pattern rules. The abstract states no cost-derived threshold or memory price.

**[18] arXiv:2401.14351, "ServerlessLLM: Low-Latency Serverless Inference for Large Language Models"**, Fu et al., 2024, OSDI 2024 ([USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/fu)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14351
- *Mechanism.* ServerlessLLM provides fast multi-tier checkpoint loading over the GPU server's storage hierarchy (DRAM, SSD) and live migration. Its "startup-time-optimized model scheduling" estimates each server's checkpoint locality and places the model where time-to-start is lowest ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14351)). The local checkpoint cache uses LRU. In the evaluation, each system's "keep-alive period [is set] equal to its loading latency, following prior work [INFaaS]" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14351)).
- *Relation to our design.* Shares the physics: load time from DRAM vs SSD grows with state size, as with r_i = bL_i/β. Differences: no forecast or timing policy, LRU retention, and a keep-alive set by a break-even rule. It reduces r rather than timing reads against arrivals, so it is complementary.

**[20] arXiv:2007.07858, "Continuous Prefetch for Interactive Data Applications"** (Khameleon), Mohammed et al., 2020, preprint record (venue not shown on the arXiv page). https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07858
- *Mechanism.* A server-side scheduler streams portions of progressively encoded responses using available bandwidth. It solves "a complex optimization based on available resources, predicted user interactions, and response quality levels" with a fast greedy approximation ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07858)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a) and (b). Shared: prefetching under a probabilistic forecast of the user's next request, and allocation of a shared resource across candidates by expected utility. Differences: the shared resource is bandwidth, not memory-time. Partial responses replace stall risk, and there is no price.

**[24] arXiv:2603.17259, "AppFlow: Memory Scheduling for Cold Launch of Large Apps on Mobile and Vehicle Systems"**, Li et al., 2026, MobiCom 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17259
- *Mechanism.* A prediction-based, system-wide scheduler integrates a Selective File Preloader, an Adaptive Memory Reclaimer and a Context-Aware Process Killer. Its premise is that "preloading reduces I/O stalls but consumes scarce memory and is undone by reclamation, while reclamation and killing free memory but sacrifice background survivability." It cuts GB-scale cold-launch latency by 66.5% ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17259)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (b) and (c). It states the same coupling the design resolves with one price: preload vs reclaim vs kill under scarce memory. Differences: heuristic coordination, with no shared price and no arrival-time distribution.

**[28] arXiv:1610.08129, "Memshare: a Dynamic Multi-tenant Memory Key-value Cache"**, Cidon et al., 2016 (arXiv), USENIX ATC 2017 as "Memshare: a Dynamic Multi-tenant Key-value Cache" ([USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc17/technical-sessions/presentation/cidon)). https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08129
- *Mechanism.* Applications get reserved memory, and the pooled remainder is allocated to maximize overall hit rate ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08129)). Following Cliffhanger, Memshare "optimizes hit rates by estimating hit rate gradients" per application. An arbiter ranks applications by need, and the log cleaner prefers segments holding data of the lowest-need applications ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc17/atc17-cidon.pdf)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (d) implicitly. Equalizing marginal hit-rate gradients is the KKT condition that a shadow price enforces. Differences: there is no explicit price, and allocation moves by gradient estimates (shadow queues) rather than dual ascent on demand minus capacity. There are no forecasts or prefetching.

**[30] arXiv:2108.06893, "Memtrade: A Disaggregated-Memory Marketplace for Public Clouds"**, Maruf et al., 2021 (arXiv), SIGMETRICS 2023 ([DOI 10.1145/3578338.3593553](https://doi.org/10.1145/3578338.3593553)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06893
- *Mechanism.* Producer VMs lease unallocated and allocated-but-idle memory to consumers, harvested by an application-aware control loop ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06893)). A broker "sets the price per unit of remote memory" for a lease time "based in part on monitoring the current price of spot instances," and the paper evaluates pricing strategies ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.06893)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (d). Memory is priced per unit and per time. Differences: this is an inter-tenant market price referenced to spot prices, not a shadow price that clears one host's capacity.

**[31] arXiv:2305.17222, "Karma: Resource Allocation for Dynamic Demands"**, Vuppalapati et al., 2023, OSDI 2023 ([USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi23/presentation/vuppalapati)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17222
- *Mechanism.* A credit-based allocator: users donate unused resources each quantum, earn credits when others borrow them, and are prioritized by credits. It gives Pareto-efficiency, strategy-proofness and fairness guarantees under dynamic demand ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17222)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (d) loosely: credits act as a currency over time. Differences: the goal is fairness among strategic users, not minimizing latency, and there is no price on idle memory-time.

**[32] arXiv:1601.06838, "A Utility Optimization Approach to Network Cache Design"**, Dehghan et al., 2016, IEEE INFOCOM 2016. Caching theory, so mentioned in passing. https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06838
- *Mechanism.* Utility-driven TTL caching: per-content timers are set so as to maximize the sum of utilities of hit probabilities under a capacity constraint. The characteristic time of LRU/FIFO "relates to the Lagrange multiplier corresponding to the cache capacity constraint," and a decentralized dual algorithm moves that multiplier by gradient steps ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.06838)). The paper also covers revenue models where publishers react to prices set by the cache provider ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.06838)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (c) and (d) closely in form: a timer derived from a capacity multiplier updated by dual gradient, citing Kelly-style congestion control ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.06838)). Differences: stationary request rates, unit-size contents, no prefetch and no timing distributions.
- **PRIOR-ART FLAG (ii).** It prices cache space with a dual-gradient (learned) shadow price. Flag it for the caching-theory researcher.

**[33] arXiv:2101.09355, "Benchmarking, Analysis, and Optimization of Serverless Function Snapshots"** (REAP), Ustiugov et al., 2021, ASPLOS 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09355
- *Mechanism.* Snapshot-started functions run 95% slower than memory-resident ones because guest memory faults in from disk one page at a time. REAP "records functions' stable working set of guest memory pages and proactively prefetches it from disk into memory," cutting cold-start delay by 3.7x on average ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09355)).
- *Relation to our design.* It shares the bulk read-back of state from storage. The difference is the trigger: REAP reads at invocation (the design's λ → ∞ "read on demand" end), with no timing ahead of arrival.

**[34] arXiv:2104.13869, "Faa$T: A Transparent Auto-Scaling Cache for Serverless Applications"**, Romero et al., 2021, ACM SoCC 2021 ([DOI 10.1145/3472883.3486974](https://doi.org/10.1145/3472883.3486974)). https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13869
- *Mechanism.* Each application's cache is unloaded from memory with the application. "Upon reloading for the next invocation, Faa$T pre-warms the cache with objects likely to be accessed," and the cache scales with working set and object sizes ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13869)). It pairs with application pre-warming "done accurately and completely off the critical invocation path" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.13869)).
- *Relation to our design.* It shares the pattern "unload state while idle, reload it ahead of use." The difference is that reload is tied to application reload, with no per-object timing rule or price.

**[37] arXiv:2610.12971, "PredictIO: Decoupling Workload Prediction from OS-Controlled Storage Prefetching"**, Lu et al., 2026, preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12971
- *Mechanism.* User-space policies receive kernel-observed accesses and application hints and emit page-range prefetch requests. A kernel executor validates, bounds and filters them before issuing asynchronous I/O. It is demonstrated for app launch, SQLite scans and LLM model loading ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12971)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a): an explicit channel through which the application tells the storage layer what will be needed. Differences: no timing distributions, cost ratio or memory price.

**[38] arXiv:2502.12826, "Ariadne: A Hotness-Aware and Size-Adaptive Compressed Swap Technique for Fast Application Relaunch and Reduced CPU Usage on Mobile Devices"**, Liang et al., 2025, HPCA 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12826
- *Mechanism.* Its proactive decompression scheme "predicts the next set of data to be used and decompresses it in advance" during app relaunch from ZRAM ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12826)).
- *Relation to our design.* Predictive staging from a slower tier (compressed memory) for a returning user, parallel to (b). The difference is that the trigger is relaunch, not a return-time forecast.

**[4] "FaasCache: keeping serverless computing alive with greedy-dual caching"**, Fuerst et al., 2021, ASPLOS 2021. [DOI 10.1145/3445814.3446757](https://doi.org/10.1145/3445814.3446757). Not on arXiv.
- *Mechanism.* Keep-alive is treated as object caching ("Keeping a function warm is equivalent" to a cache hit). Cost reflects the function's initialization/running time and Size its memory footprint ([PDF](https://homes.luddy.indiana.edu/prateeks/papers/faascache-asplos21.pdf)). Hit-ratio curves and proportional control size the server's memory (vertical auto-scaling) ([PDF](https://homes.luddy.indiana.edu/prateeks/papers/faascache-asplos21.pdf)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (c), plus (d) through sizing. Shared: a size- and cost-aware eviction index that degenerates to recency (LRU-like). The design's index likewise reduces to LRU with no forecast information. Differences: no idle-time forecast (frequency stands in for return likelihood), no pre-warming, and no price. **[5] "Informed mobile prefetching"** (IMP), Higgins et al., 2012, ACM MobiSys 2012. [DOI 10.1145/2307636.2307651](https://doi.org/10.1145/2307636.2307651). Not on arXiv.
- *Mechanism.* Applications disclose prefetch hints. IMP "uses a cost-benefit analysis to decide when to prefetch data" and "employs goal-directed adaptation to try to minimize application response time while meeting budgets for battery lifetime and cellular data usage." It tracks the hit rates of past prefetches to adapt to hint accuracy ([abstract](https://solar.cs.ucl.ac.uk/appoptimization.github.io/publications/HFG2012)). Per a third-party lecture deck summarizing the paper (secondary source): "conversion rates" make power and network resources comparable to time. A "control loop changes [the] conversion rate of [a] budgeted resource," checking the remaining supply once a second as in Odyssey's goal-directed adaptation, with the update c_new = c_old × c_adjustment. Expected costs are discounted by hint accuracy (hints consumed / hints total), and IMP prefetches over a network whose benefit minus cost is positive ([WPI slides on IMP](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/mobile-and-ubiquitous-computing-informed-mobile-ppt-presentation-894543)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a), (b) and (d). Shared: client-disclosed forecasts (hints) with tracked accuracy, prefetching when benefit exceeds priced cost, and prices adapted by feedback to keep consumption at a budget. Per the secondary source the adaptation is multiplicative, like the design's λ ← λe^{ηg}. Differences: the budgets are energy and data over a horizon, not instantaneous memory capacity. Hints say what will be used, not when, so there is no lead-time or stall-risk threshold. No retention or eviction is involved.
- **PRIOR-ART FLAG (ii), the closest systems precedent for a feedback-learned resource price driving prefetch decisions.** The details of the price update come from a secondary source. The primary full text was not accessible (see Gaps).

**[6] "An adaptive network prefetch scheme"**, Jiang et al. (Z. Jiang, L. Kleinrock), 1998, IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications. [DOI 10.1109/49.669044](https://doi.org/10.1109/49.669044). Not on arXiv.
- *Mechanism.* A prediction module estimates each file's probability of being requested soon. A threshold module computes a per-server prefetch threshold, and files are prefetched when their access probability is at least the threshold. "We derive a formula for the prefetch threshold to determine its value dynamically based on system load, capacity, and the cost of time and system resources to the user." Prefetching above the threshold "always" lowers average cost ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/49.669044)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (b), and (d) through the load dependence. Shared: a probability threshold derived from the ratio of time cost to resource cost, rising with load. Differences: the probability concerns whether a file is requested "in the near future," with no lead time or arrival-time distribution. The resource is network and server capacity, not memory-time. The threshold comes from a model rather than a learned price.
- **PRIOR-ART FLAG (i), for "prefetch iff probability ≥ cost-derived threshold."** Cite it as the classic precedent. The design's novelty is the timed, size-dependent critical ratio over the read window.

**[7] "ORION and the Three Rights: Sizing, Bundling, and Prewarming for Serverless DAGs"**, Mahgoub et al., 2022, OSDI 2022. [USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/mahgoub). Not on arXiv.
- *Mechanism.* A performance model keeps full latency distributions (CDFs) of functions in a DAG. Pre-warming delays d for later stages are chosen as d* = argmin E2E-Latency(P, d) subject to Util(P, d) ≥ a target utilization "set (and dynamically adjusted) by the provider." Util = busy/(busy + idle) of the pre-warmed VM, and the search is best-first in 100 ms increments ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi22-mahgoub.pdf)). The first stage gets d_1 = 0 "because pre-warming requires predicting when the DAG will be invoked, which is challenging in the general case" ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi22-mahgoub.pdf)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (b), with a capacity-like constraint as in (d). Shared: pre-warm timing chosen from full distributions to trade latency against idle reserved resources. Differences: it times intra-DAG stages from upstream latency distributions, whereas the design times arrivals after external tool gaps. Waste is controlled by a provider-set utilization target and search, not a price, and costs are not size-dependent.
- Weak **PRIOR-ART FLAG (i)**: distribution-based pre-warm timing via constrained optimization, but not a cost ratio.

**[8] "IceBreaker: warming serverless functions better with heterogeneity"**, Roy et al., 2022, ASPLOS 2022. [DOI 10.1145/3503222.3507750](https://doi.org/10.1145/3503222.3507750). Not on arXiv.
- *Mechanism.* A Fast Fourier Transform predictor (quadratic trend plus the top 10 harmonics over a one-hour local window, evaluated each one-minute interval) forecasts invocation concurrency ([PDF](http://www1.ece.neu.edu/~ningfang/SimPaper/icebreaker-ASPLOS22.pdf)). A utility score S_u = [T_n + (1 − F_p) + (1 − I_s) + (1 − M_r)]/4 combines the true-negative rate, false-positive rate, inter-server speedup and memory footprint, each min-max normalized. If S_u is above the high-end cut-off the function is warmed on a high-end server, between the cut-offs on a low-end server, and below them not at all ([PDF](http://www1.ece.neu.edu/~ningfang/SimPaper/icebreaker-ASPLOS22.pdf)). The cut-offs start at 2/3 and 1/3 and "change dynamically ... in proportion to the fraction of vacant memory on the servers." The paper reports 45% lower keep-alive cost and 27% lower execution time ([PDF](http://www1.ece.neu.edu/~ningfang/SimPaper/icebreaker-ASPLOS22.pdf)).
- *Relation to our design.* Parallels (a) and (c), and weakly (d). Shared: a memory-footprint penalty in the warm decision, and thresholds that respond to memory vacancy (a congestion signal). Differences: the score is an equal-weight heuristic, the cut-offs are tuned and nudged rather than derived from a cost ratio, and the forecast is per-interval concurrency rather than a time-to-arrival distribution.

### Inferences
- **Precise relation to [1] (my derivation, not the paper's).** The design's retain rule μ_i(t)·r_i·(1 + λL_i) ≥ λL_i is the sign condition of [1]'s Theorem 2, c_cs·λ(x|H) ≥ c_p, under the mapping c_p = λL_i (price × size) and c_cs = r_i(1 + λL_i) (stall plus memory held during the read). For short reads F_i(r_i|t) ≈ μ_i(t)·r_i, so the fetch rule's start time coincides with [1]'s pre-warm boundary for an increasing hazard. The design departs from [1] in three ways: (1) when the read is long relative to how fast the hazard changes, because the risk integrates over the read window and the read holds memory in flight (the newsvendor form); (2) when λ is endogenous and learned from capacity; (3) when costs scale with L_i. The paper should state this lineage explicitly rather than let a reviewer find it.
- **What appears new relative to verified work:** (i) a stall-risk threshold q = λL/(1+λL) that depends jointly on state size and a shared capacity price (none of [1]–[3], [6] or [8] has both); (ii) the timed critical ratio over a finite, size-proportional read window, whereas others pre-warm instantaneously ([1]), use percentiles ([2]) or use deterministic slack ([3], [7]); (iii) a capacity shadow price learned by multiplicative dual ascent in a memory system, where verified systems use auctions ([25]), market bids ([26]), fixed taxes ([11], [27]), gradient hill-climbing ([28], [29]), broker pricing ([30]) or credits ([31]); (iv) client-supplied, per-tool-key distributions conditioned on elapsed idle time, whereas others learn per-function histories ([1], [2], [3]).
- **Likely reviewer citations, in order of risk:** [1] for (b)/(c); [3] for (c); [6] for (b); [5] for (d) (a feedback-learned price with a multiplicative update, per the secondary source); [32] for (d) (dual-gradient cache price, theory); [26] and [11] for "memory or cache as a priced resource."
- The serverless-LLM loading line ([18], [42]) is complementary. It shrinks r_i (faster formats, multicast, retention in GPU memory), which in the design shrinks x_i and therefore how early reads must start. It does not compete on timing.
- The agentic analogs ([15] SpecBox, [44] JointServe, [43] Crab) show the same "use the generation or tool gap to stage something" idea arriving in 2026. Only the design prices memory and conditions on tool-duration distributions.

## Q1. Serverless cold starts: which policies pre-warm and keep alive instances using idle-time distributions, what forecast does each use, how are thresholds chosen, and is any derived from a cost model?

### Takeaway
Production-inspired serverless policies forecast with per-function idle-time histograms ([2], [3], [16]), time-series or FFT models ([2]'s ARIMA, [8], [13]) or Bayesian/RL models ([12], [14]). Almost all set their cut-offs by tuning against cold-start vs wasted-memory trade-offs. The exceptions with an explicit cost model are [1] (hazard-rate condition c_cs·λ(x) = c_p), [3] (minimizing D·C_keep + C_cold·P(τ > D)), [7] (latency minimized subject to a utilization target), [13] (MPC objective) and [14] (an RL reward with a carbon price). None ties the threshold to the state's size and a learned capacity price. Serverless-LLM work ([18], [42]) mostly shortens load time rather than timing loads.

### Cited Findings
- **Hybrid histogram [2].** Forecast: per-application idle-time histogram (1-min bins, 4-h range), with ARIMA for out-of-bound idle times. Thresholds: 5th/99th percentiles with a 10% margin and CV ≥ 2 for representativeness, chosen by sensitivity study and Pareto curves of cold starts vs wasted memory time ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)). Cost model: none; it is evaluated on a two-metric Pareto frontier ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc20-shahrad.pdf)).
- **Keep-alive as caching [4] (FaasCache).** Forecast: none explicit; invocation frequency plus a recency clock. Index: Clock + Freq × Cost / Size, terminating the lowest priority only under resource pressure ([PDF](https://homes.luddy.indiana.edu/prateeks/papers/faascache-asplos21.pdf)). Cost model: Greedy-Dual's cost/size ratio, but nothing sets a keep-alive time.
- **IceBreaker [8].** Forecast: FFT (10 harmonics plus quadratic trend, 1-hour window, 1-minute intervals). Thresholds: equal-weight utility score with cut-offs 2/3 and 1/3, adjusted in proportion to vacant memory ([PDF](http://www1.ece.neu.edu/~ningfang/SimPaper/icebreaker-ASPLOS22.pdf)). Cost model: none; it is a heuristic score.
- **Theory of keep-alive [1].** Forecast: history-dependent point-process (Poisson/Hawkes) hazard. Thresholds: derived as the points where c_p = c_cs·λ(x|H), yielding pre-warm and keep-alive windows ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03521)). This is the clearest cost-ratio derivation found (PRIOR-ART FLAG (i)).
- **GearUp [3].** Forecast: histogram blended with a short-term predictor, conditioned on parent DAG state. Threshold: D* = argmin D·C_keep + C_cold·P(τ > D) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38196)). Cost model: yes, with fixed unit costs (PRIOR-ART FLAG (i)).
- **ORION [7].** Forecast: per-function latency distributions in a DAG performance model. Pre-warm delays are chosen by best-first search to minimize E2E latency subject to a provider-set utilization target. The DAG's first stage is never pre-warmed because its invocation time is hard to predict ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi22-mahgoub.pdf)).
- **Market keep-alive [9].** Customers bid their cold-start cost, and keep-alive lengths are learned by online learning over experts with Myerson or externality payments ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03091)).
- **Aquatope [12]:** Bayesian models create pre-warmed containers ahead of invocations under uncertainty ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13882)). **MPC [13]:** Fourier forecasts feed a constrained optimization of prewarming, reclamation and dispatch ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07640)). **LACE-RL [14]:** RL keep-alive with a carbon-intensity price ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23935)). **SPES [16]:** category-specific pre-load/unload rules ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17574)). **RL [43]:** Q-learning pre-initialization ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07541)).
- **Function chains:** Xanadu [35] provisions speculatively and just in time along chains ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/3423211.3425690)).
- **Serverless LLM loading.**
  - ServerlessLLM [18] uses multi-tier checkpoint loading and locality-aware startup-time scheduling ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14351)), LRU for local checkpoint caches, and in evaluation a keep-alive equal to loading latency, following INFaaS ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14351)).
  - λScale (RDMA multicast, execute-while-load) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09922)), BlitzScale (network-based loading, O(1) host caching) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17246)) and HydraServe (proactive model distribution) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15524)) speed up or relocate loading.
  - DeepServe uses pre-warmed pods and DRAM pre-loading ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14417)). Torpor keeps models in host memory and swaps them in on arrival ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03622)). Tangram retains parameters in idle GPU memory ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01357)). Medusa materializes the KV-init and CUDA-graph stages ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/3669940.3707285)).
- **Agentic sandboxes:** SpecBox [15] pre-warms tool sandboxes from mid-generation intent detection, using tuned keyword thresholds and Markov prefetching under a budget ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23933)).

### Inferences
- The serverless literature converges on the design's two decisions: pre-warm time and keep-alive length. It sets them almost entirely with tuned percentiles or heuristic scores. The design's position: the percentile should be the critical ratio q = λL/(1+λL), which [2] fixes at one value for all functions and [1] derives only for a fixed holding cost with instantaneous loads.
- Load duration is treated as a fixed cold-start penalty in every serverless policy found ([1], [2], [3], [8]). None models a load that occupies memory while in flight and must start x seconds before arrival. Serverless-LLM systems, where load time is large and size-proportional, reduce it instead of scheduling around it ([18], [42]).
- Memory size enters keep-alive only as an eviction weight ([4]: Freq × Cost / Size; [8]: M_r term). No system found makes the stall risk a function of size.

### Gaps
- Exact pre-warming decision rules for Aquatope, SPES and INFless (LSTH) were not read from the full texts.
- I found no serverless system that learns a memory price online from demand vs capacity. IceBreaker's cut-off adjustment by vacant memory [8] is the closest, and it is a heuristic.

## Q2. User-driven prefetching: which systems prefetch for mobile, web, video or app launch when the user's return is uncertain, and how do they set thresholds or budgets?

### Takeaway
Mobile and web prefetching has explicit cost-benefit machinery. IMP [5] weighs prefetch benefit against energy and data costs converted by conversion rates that a control loop adapts to meet budgets. Jiang & Kleinrock [6] derive a load- and cost-dependent probability threshold for prefetching. FALCON [21] and APPM [22] predict which app and when, and preload. Dashlet [19] and Khameleon [20] fetch under probabilistic models of user behavior. None times fetches with a size-dependent critical ratio against a holding cost for memory.

### Cited Findings
- **IMP [5].** Cost-benefit prefetch decisions with "goal-directed adaptation" to meet battery and cellular-data budgets, adapting to hint accuracy ([abstract](https://solar.cs.ucl.ac.uk/appoptimization.github.io/publications/HFG2012)). Per a secondary lecture deck: adaptive conversion rates with c_new = c_old × c_adjustment, checks once per second, and costs discounted by accuracy (hints consumed / hints total) ([WPI slides](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/mobile-and-ubiquitous-computing-informed-mobile-ppt-presentation-894543)). Lineage: Odyssey's OS-guided energy adaptation to meet battery-duration goals ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/319151.319155)).
- **Adaptive network prefetch [6].** Prefetch files whose access probability is at least a per-server threshold derived "based on system load, capacity, and the cost of time and system resources to the user" ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/49.669044)). Predictive web prefetching (server predicts, clients prefetch) trades latency for traffic ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/235160.235164)).
- **App pre-launch.** FALCON preloads apps and fresh content from context-based predictions with a cost-benefit learning algorithm, saving about 6 s per launch at ≤ 2% battery per day ([MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=162635)). APPM predicts which app and when, and prefetches on screen unlock while accounting for bandwidth costs ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/2493432.2493490)). AppFlow coordinates a selective preloader with reclaim and kill under scarce memory ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17259)). SmartLMK keeps apps by modeled launch-time cost and usage ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/2894755)).
- **Video and interactive data.** Dashlet pre-buffers by expected rebuffering under a swipe-time model and is robust to 50% distribution error ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi23-li-zhuqi.pdf)). Multi-video prefetching for short video is formulated as a challenge problem ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09839)). Khameleon streams response portions from predicted interactions via a greedy utility optimization ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07858)). Bulk ad prefetching conflicts with real-time ad auctions ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/2465351.2465378)).
- **Release on predicted idleness:** TOP releases the radio when apps predict a long idle time ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/icnp.2010.5762777)).
- **Agentic:** SpecBox pre-warms tool sandboxes from intent predictions under a budget ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23933)).

### Inferences
- IMP is the best systems precedent for (d): a resource price learned by feedback to meet a budget, with the price turning each prefetch into a local benefit-vs-cost test, which is exactly how the design decouples sessions. The differences are the resource (energy and data budgets over hours vs instantaneous memory capacity) and the forecast (whether, not when).
- Jiang & Kleinrock is the classic precedent for a cost-derived probability threshold. The design generalizes it by measuring the probability over the read's own duration and conditioning on idle time.

### Gaps
- IMP's primary text and FALCON's cost-benefit algorithm were not read (see Q0 Gaps).
- I found no mobile or web system whose prefetch threshold scales with object size through a holding cost.

## Q3. Price-based allocation: which systems allocate memory or cache by price, which use dual-decomposition or congestion-pricing controllers, and how is the price updated?

### Takeaway
Verified systems price memory or cache in five ways, but none runs a dual-ascent controller on a host-memory price:
- auctions or markets that clear each round from bids: Ginseng (VEE'14, LLC version ATC'16) and XChange (HPCA'15);
- a fixed "price" from shares plus an idle-memory tax: VMware ESX (OSDI'02);
- tax-and-redistribute by a tail-latency metric: RobinHood (OSDI'18);
- marginal hit-rate gradient equalization: Cliffhanger (NSDI'16) and Memshare (ATC'17);
- broker prices tied to spot prices (Memtrade) or credits (Karma).

The only verified dual-gradient shadow price on cache capacity is in caching theory (Dehghan et al., INFOCOM'16). The closest systems precedent for a feedback-learned price is IMP's adaptive conversion rates for energy and data.

### Cited Findings
- **Auctions and markets.** Ginseng continuously collects truthful bids and reallocates memory, reaching 83–100% of optimum ([abstract](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/ginseng-market-driven-memory-allocation/)). Ginseng-LLC auctions cache ways ([USENIX abstract](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc16/technical-sessions/presentation/funaro)). In XChange, "each shared resource is assigned a price which changes over time," with allocation proportional to bids ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/hpca.2015.7056026)). REF derives fair cache and bandwidth shares from Cobb-Douglas utilities ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/2644865.2541962)).
- **Shares as price with an idle tax (ESX).** The "shares-per-page ratio can be interpreted as a price." Idle pages are charged more through a tax rate that defaults to 75%, and active fraction is estimated by sampling ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/osdi02/tech/full_papers/waldspurger/waldspurger.pdf)). The price is static and administrator-set.
- **Tax-and-redistribute (RobinHood).** Every Δ seconds it reclaims 1% of each backend's cache and redistributes it in proportion to the request blocking count ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi18-berger.pdf)).
- **Gradient equalization.** Cliffhanger iteratively reallocates across and within applications ([USENIX abstract](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi16/technical-sessions/presentation/cidon)). Memshare uses hit-rate gradients to rank need and evicts from the lowest-need applications ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc17/atc17-cidon.pdf)).
- **Broker pricing and credits.** Memtrade's broker sets per-unit remote-memory prices per lease "based in part on monitoring the current price of spot instances" ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.06893)). Karma allocates by credits earned when donating idle resources ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17222)).
- **Keep-alive markets and exogenous prices.** Custom keep-alive policies charge Myerson or externality payments on bids of cold-start cost ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03091)). LACE-RL prices idle time by grid carbon intensity ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23935)).
- **Dual decomposition (theory).** Utility-driven TTL caching ties the capacity constraint's Lagrange multiplier to LRU's characteristic time and updates it by gradient on the dual, citing Kelly-style congestion control ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.06838)).
- **Feedback-learned prices (non-memory).** IMP's control loop adjusts the conversion rates of budgeted energy and data (secondary source: multiplicative update) ([WPI slides](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/mobile-and-ubiquitous-computing-informed-mobile-ppt-presentation-894543)).
- **Fair, non-price cache sharing:** ROBUS gives a proportionally fair, core-satisfying allocation via randomization over time batches ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06736)).

### Inferences
- The design's update λ ← λ·exp(η(d − R)/R) is, in memory systems, closest to XChange's time-varying prices and IMP's control loop, and is formally Dehghan et al.'s dual gradient (multiplicative rather than additive). Citing Kelly (already in the design) plus [32] and [5] covers the lineage. [26] and [11] answer "memory has been priced before."
- Counting refused reads and preempted staged reads as demand is what makes the slope observable at a hard limit. None of the verified systems describes an equivalent measurement. RobinHood's RBC and Memshare's gradients are the closest observable proxies.

### Gaps
- I did not find a production or research memory manager that explicitly runs dual ascent on a memory price driven by measured excess demand; this absence is from my searches, not proof.
- XChange's exact price-update rule beyond "changes over time" with proportional allocation was not read.

## Q4. Predictive tiering: which tiered-memory or storage systems page data in ahead of a predicted access using probabilistic or timing predictions?

### Takeaway
Most tiering systems are reactive (hotness and demotion: TPP, xTier) or pattern-predictive (majority stride: Leap; pre-recorded traces: 3PO; recorded working sets: REAP, FaaSnap, Picocenter; ML reuse or admission prediction: Baleen, Flashield, Sibyl; application hints: TIP, PredictIO). Their reads are triggered at or after the access that needs them (restore on invocation or relaunch). None verified times a bulk read-ahead against a probability distribution of when the next access will arrive, or prices the fast tier's memory-time.

### Cited Findings
- **Restore-time working-set prefetch.**
  - REAP records the stable working set and prefetches it from disk on cold start, 3.7x faster ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09355)).
  - FaaSnap uses loading-set files and concurrent paging ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/3492321.3524270)).
  - Picocenter's ActiveSet prefetches the predicted working set when reviving a swapped-out app, after a 1-minute idle swap-out ([PDF](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~dml/papers/picocenter_eurosys16.pdf)).
  - Faa$T pre-warms a per-app cache on reload ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13869)).
  - Ariadne proactively decompresses predicted relaunch data ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12826)).
  - AppFlow selectively preloads files for predicted launches ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17259)).
- **Pattern- or hint-based prefetch.** Leap uses majority-based remote-memory prefetch ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09829)). 3PO pre-plans prefetching for oblivious applications ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07688)). PredictIO passes application hints to a kernel prefetch executor ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12971)). TIP uses hint-driven cost-benefit buffer allocation ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/224056.224064)).
- **Learned placement or admission.** Baleen uses ML admission and prefetch optimizing Disk-head Time under a write-rate budget ([USENIX abstract](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast24/presentation/wong)). Flashield admits objects to SSD when ML predicts frequent reads without updates ([USENIX abstract](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi19/presentation/eisenman)). Sibyl uses RL placement in hybrid storage ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07394)). Pond predicts local vs pool memory per VM ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00241)). xTier scores pages with an in-kernel MLP ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27266)). TPP demotes proactively to keep headroom ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02878)).
- **Pre-writing to the slow tier.** Marvin's ahead-of-time swap pre-writes memory to disk for fast reclaim ([USENIX abstract](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc20/presentation/lebeck)).
- **Tiered model loading for serverless LLMs:** loading over DRAM/SSD with locality-aware scheduling and LRU checkpoint caches ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14351), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14351)).

### Inferences
- The design's (b) appears to have no direct analog in tiering systems: they decide what to bring in, not when to start a long, size-proportional read relative to an uncertain arrival. The design's (c) has partial analogs in value-per-space policies (LHD's hit density; FaasCache's cost/size), which use no idle-time-conditioned return forecasts.
- Picocenter's setting (mostly-idle long-lived apps with state on cloud storage) is the closest structural precedent for the design's problem. It suggests a framing: "Picocenter swaps in on arrival; we schedule the swap-in before arrival under a memory price."

### Gaps
- Database buffer pools: I found no verified buffer-pool system that prefetches against timing distributions. A 2025 survey (arXiv:2512.22995, seen only in an OpenAlex listing and not opened) could be checked.
- CXL and tiered-memory work is dominated by hotness-based placement. I found no timing-predictive CXL prefetcher among the verified records.
