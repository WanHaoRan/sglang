# fetch

## summary
The engine decides when an SSD read starts in two places. Before arrival, each plan is checked every 0.5 s. just_in_time starts the lookup once now >= predicted arrival + W - r - (early_s + 1 s). The predicted arrival comes from the exact gap or the entry's p50, and early_s = p50 - p10, so jit_p50 lands its reads at the p10. It never conditions on idle time. expected_cost conditions the entry's 64-point lognormal support on idle time and picks the start that minimises stall + lambda x room tokens x pin time. It reads early only when r > W. In both rules W is the wait a turn arriving now would see: (queue length + 0.5) / admissions per second over the last 60 s, falling back to 300 s, then to a wait EWMA. It is a point value, not projected to the arrival time, and EC counts the queue only while the GPU is the bottleneck. r = 0.9 s of fixed lags + read-thread ms per page x (own pages + pages already dispatched), plus a 1 s margin. After arrival the turn looks up at once. It joins any staged read; the arrival gate skips turns whose history is already resident and caps lookups at 3. The read is then held until (position + 0.5)/rate - r - 2 s, except at queue positions 0-1. Here r counts the parked reads ahead of it as well. The read queue dispatches by earliest deadline, 'now' reads first, within a 512-page window and a budget of half the host pool. Timed reads stop while pending writes hold 50K tokens. A landed read stays pinned until admission, within a pin budget of 0.3 x the host pool. No phase schedules backward from deadlines, and the pre-arrival r leaves out parked reads and the write hold. In campaign 34, reads the hold released at once still waited a median 30 s parked. On FA3 the best arms barely read before arrival (ec_full 1 read, jit_exact 99, jit_p50 1,208), and the price never moved. On the trace's heavy-tailed gap entries, the paper's threshold F(x|t) >= q would start reads that the engine's own cost function rejects (CPU check). One rule P(G + W <= now + r) >= q would plug in at _check_pending before arrival and at read_not_before / read_deadline after it, where trigger_time is already shared. It needs a distribution of W projected to the arrival time, r taken from the storage queue (parked + in flight + write hold), and a guard against gaps whose hazard falls with idle time.

## discrepancies
- Paper Fetch, eq. fetch: the engine never evaluates F_i(x_i|t) >= q_i = lambda L/(1 + lambda L). It minimises expected cost over the 64 support points (next_turn_policy.py:165-201), and the two disagree on the trace's unimodal, heavy-tailed entries, not only on multi-modal ones. On the tool entry the threshold says read now, at a cost of 1.81 s against 1.10 s at arrival (sim/cost_curve.py). The paper's justification ('F rises as the session stays idle') fails whenever the hazard falls with idle time.
- Paper Fetch, r_i = bL_i/beta: the code's r adds 0.9 s of fixed lags (alloc 0.5 s, drain 0.36 s, fixed 0.044 s), a 1 s margin and the pages of reads already in flight. The per-page cost comes from the read thread's EWMA (median 3.5 ms/page in c34), not from bL/beta (next_turn_estimators.py:119-144; next_turn_prefetch.py:1256-1258).
- Paper Fetch, W_hat 'from its position in the queue and the admission rate': before arrival the position is the whole current queue (JIT), or 0 unless the GPU is the bottleneck (EC, next_turn_prefetch.py:698-712). It is not projected to the arrival time, falls back to a 300 s window and then a wait EWMA, and is a point value.
- Paper Fetch, memory held = L_i: EC charges rent on room_tokens (key tokens not on the GPU), while the read time uses missing tokens (next_turn_prefetch.py:1153-1155, 1161-1166). A parked read holds no pages, only an anchor lock; pages are taken at dispatch.
- Paper 'When to plan' (plan when the gap begins, replan when an input changes): the code re-runs the decision every 0.5 s while a plan is pending (next_turn_prefetch.py:997, 1174).
- Paper J_i includes recompute rho U(2L - U): read_start's cost has only the turn's own stall (1 s per s) and rent. There is no recompute term and no cost to the requests queued behind.
- The paper's Fetch section covers only pre-arrival starts and says a read 'started at arrival' completes before the head of the queue. The engine does not read at arrival: it looks up at arrival and holds the read until (position + 0.5)/rate - r - 2 s (positions >= 2). It also uses earliest-deadline dispatch, a 512-page window, a budget of half the host pool, a pin budget of 0.3 x the host pool, the write hold, the arrival gate and refetch with a 3-lookup cap. None of this is in the paper, though it is the queue-delay-based schedule the user credits with the gain.
- Paper Price section: the arms use 'harm' learning (faded harm / faded staged token-seconds, next_turn_policy.py:290-291), not the multiplicative update on demand minus capacity ('room'). There is no cold-start bisection, no recalibration of forecast tails, and no counting of evicted caches with nu >= lambda. Observed, lambda never moved in c32 and c34 ec_full (5e-5) and fell to its 1e-7 floor in c32 ec_exact.

## open questions
- Should the unified rule keep a global comparison with 'read at arrival' (an argmin, as read_start does) rather than a pure threshold? With T = G + W and a gap hazard that falls with idle time, P(T <= now + r) >= q fires right after the turn ends and pins memory through the tail.
- How should W become a distribution? The ntp_admit lines already log predicted (pred_wait_ql_ms, pred_wait_ewma_ms) against measured waits for every planned turn. Calibrating the error from campaigns 32/34 is a CPU-only step, not done here.
- Should W count only the queued turns admission will not pass over, those not waiting on their own reads? The tick already computes this for room retry (next_turn_prefetch.py:661-667). Raw position over-counts the turns ahead when many queued turns are waiting on reads.
- Should r include the parked queue and the write hold? If so, how should the length of a write hold be predicted? In c34 holds lasted a median 2.0-2.8 s, max 5.8-8.2 s.
- Is a single pre-arrival rule worth its complexity on this trace? Campaign 34's best arms staged 1 (ec_full) and 99 (jit_exact) reads before arrival, against roughly 2,200-2,700 reads in all. The early-read gain is visible only at lighter load with exact hints (campaign 26: 0.14-0.21 s).
- The only early read ec_full made in c34 came at 1,198 s of idle. That is where the client's gap cap (GAP_CAP=1200 in campaign34/settings.env) and MAX_PLANNED_GAP_S=1200 both put probability mass. Is it a real forecast or an artifact of the two caps?
- Why did reads the hold released at once still wait a median 30 s parked in c34? The census points to the 512-page window, with the read thread saturated, but ReadSchedulerStats (window, budget and write holds) is never logged, so it is unconfirmed.
- RECOMPUTE_S_PER_TOKEN = 1.1e-4 (Triton, Qwen3-30B-A3B) drives the harm price. Any GPU-time cost in a new design should use the FA3 figure of 0.042-0.047 ms/token for Qwen3-32B-FP8.
