# evidence

## summary
The records back the user's statement only in part. Almost all of the measured gain over HiCache comes from the queue-based read schedule: reads timed to predicted admission minus read time, pinned until admission, with queued turns' history kept. At 44 users / 70 GB (Triton, x10), pins alone give 3.8 s (79 % of the gap to exact hints), and the queue-state mechanisms add about 1.0 s more. A no-hint engine with both reaches 96 % of the way to exact hints. At 128 users / 160 GB the no-hint queue-aware arm is at 12.07 s, against 27.74 s for the pins-only arm (one run each). On TraceLab, where the queue wait W (about 170-260 s) is far above the read time r (about 3-6 s) and the read line is short, the hold is worth -77.7 s [-85.1, -70.4]. Where the read thread itself is saturated (campaign 28, reads parked 51-66 s), the read line does the timing and the arms tie. Reads before arrival show a gain in one setting only: 44 users / 70 GB, Triton, exact hints, where most turns outside load episodes have W < r. There exact hints are 0.14-0.21 s ahead of no hints (about 3-4 % of the HiCache gap), against a ceiling of 0.14-0.20 s of read-only stall. With realistic hints no gain is shown. A p50 point estimate costs 0.6-0.9 s (campaign 26 and FA3 campaign 34). On FA3 without the write fixes it started the refused-read loop 8-17 minutes early (8.6 s). At saturation, or with 100 GB of host memory, early reads add nothing. Where eviction by forecast was isolated it moved TTFT by 0.1 s at most; protecting queued turns is what mattered. The four write-path fixes are worth 5.8-9.5 s on FA3. At 44 users, 30 GB more host memory (0.52 s for the no-hint arm) bought more than any hint did. On question 2: after the first cleaner pass (minute 87.9-88.3), the three campaign 34 arms had 116 returning requests that lost at least 1,024 history tokens in 22 minutes, against 16 in the 22 minutes before. Of the 116, 65 had every history lookup find 0 (48 starting at the session's first page), 18 found a contiguous run that stopped partway, 17 found the whole history but then hit a deleted file on the read (31 such failures, all after a pass), and 16 made no history lookup. The recompute comes in bursts after each pass (up to 292K tokens a minute) with 2-5K minutes in between. It totals 1.1-1.3 M tokens per arm (1.5-1.7 % of history). The late rise in TTFT starts at minute 85-86, when the 128-user cap binds, before the first pass. Several numbers in the design note have no record behind them: the Nov 20 trace study, 974 of 2,065, and +0.003 s. Others need qualifying: the 0.16 s, the W < r label, the 0.86 s recompute, the 166K, and "rose until the end".

## discrepancies
- Design note §1 regimes, 'Recorded pace': 'Human turns hold 76-95 % of resume read time; per-tool forecasts hide 0-0.6 % of it (Nov 20 trace study)'. No record holds this. I searched the .md/.out/.txt/.json files under agent_cache/ (outside frozen/), REALISTIC_HINTS.md, MAINSTREAM_REPLAY.md, the campaign 27-29 checks, earlier session scratch folders and the memory notes. REALISTIC_HINTS.md:137 has a different claim: a per-tool hint keeps 50-54 % of the oracle's eviction gain at x70.
- Design note §3: 'about half of its landed reads were evicted before use (974 of 2,065 and 1,209 of 2,139 in two runs)'. These counts are in no record. The recorded ones are 979 of 2,009 and 1,144 of 2,032 turns (campaign25/compare_h_rep.out mechanism rows) and 973 of 2,047 (campaign23/gain44/v1_ladder_lost.out). 2,067 and 2,139 are the two HiCache runs' s2h_io key counts (campaign26/checks/verify/method/census_report.out). 'About half' holds.
- Design note §3: 'The index alone moved mean TTFT by +0.003 s (campaign 26)'. The record gives rule 3 alone against JIT p50 as +0.004 s [-0.11, +0.12] on 6,715 turns and -0.019 s on 9,690 (campaign26/DECISIONS.md:967-969). The +0.003 in pairs_ec.out is ec_r1 against rules 1+3, a different pair. Also, the engine's return_chance is the chance of return within 30 s alone; the paper's retention index nu_i (the (1+lambda L) factor and the max over H) was never built (campaign26/DECISIONS.md:60).
- Design note §3: return-chance eviction 'was 0.16 s slower' in campaign 32. Without the outside-request stall it is 0.07 s [-0.24, +0.10] (campaign32/DECISIONS.md:59-64). The pair (ec_exact against jit_exact) also differs in read timing (expected_cost against just_in_time), price and soft pins, so the difference is not eviction alone.
- Design note §1 regimes: campaign 32 minutes 30-70 labelled 'W < r'. The records give queue waits of 5.8-7.1 s against 0.8-1.1 s to read 20K tokens uncontended, which is W > r (mechanisms_findings.txt:178; campaign32/DECISIONS.md:253-254). The engine's own estimates at ec_full's 'wait' decisions were 3.81 s against 4.48 s, so W and r were about equal (mechanisms_findings.txt:104). The record's mechanism is that the short read line no longer delays early reads (mechanisms_findings.txt:214), not that W < r.
- Design note §2 table: 'Queue wait (mean) 23.32 s vs 4.17 s' is only the part 'queued, not its own read'. The whole queue wait is 32.63 s against 5.00 s (campaign32/DECISIONS.md:109-113).
- Design note §2: 'With an empty queue on FA3, reading 20K tokens takes 0.8-1.1 s against 0.86 s to recompute them'. The 0.86 s is the recompute cost under load. Idle cold prompts cost 0.031 ms a token, about 0.62 s for 20K, so on an idle FA3 GPU recompute beats the read (mechanisms_findings.txt:174).
- Design note §2: 'one recompute cost about 80 times what a per-session model charges' and 'the GPU serves requests in arrival order' are inferred, not measured. schedule_policy is fcfs, but wait_complete admission passes over requests whose read has not landed (campaign26/DECISIONS.md:72-75). The closest measured chain is campaign 28's: 5 points more GPU time spent on recomputed history, 5 % fewer requests, 6.7 % longer waits (campaign28/DECISIONS.md:364-367).
- Design note §3 'Recency is a weak proxy ... not supported at recorded pace ... idle age gives nearly the right order': no record measures eviction order at x1. At x10 and 70 GB, exact next use beats LRU by 7.9 points of reading turns (campaign26/DECISIONS.md:1113-1114). What the records do show is that the three-quantile score does no better than LRU with ended sessions first (13.6 against 13.7 %). The same applies to class 3's 'at recorded pace the order is close to idle age'.
- Design note §3: 'Campaign 32's first refused reads came while pending writes held up to 166K tokens'. 166K is jit_p50's window peak for minutes 30-60 (campaign34/DECISIONS.md:95-97). At the first refusals of the early-reading arms, pins plus in-flight reads held 207-393K against 33-68K of pending writes. The sustained-loop refusals began with 57-158K pending. 0.25-0.5M of host room is accounted for by no logged lock, and the verifiers found that neither pins nor write locks explain the pre-loop refusals by size (campaign32/checks/mechanisms_findings.txt, early_onset C4 and the verify ALTERNATIVES).
- Design note §3: 'With rewrites skipped and writes merged, pending writes stayed under 90K'. Campaign 34 ran all four fixes together and cannot say which one kept pending writes down (campaign34/DECISIONS.md:95-97,166-167).
- Design note §3 and campaign34/DECISIONS.md:103-105 ('until the end'; 'what remains late ... from minute 88, history the SSD cleaner deleted'): the 290K peak is right (jit_exact, minute 87: 292K). But the recompute comes in bursts after each pass, with 2-5K minutes between them, and totals only 1.14-1.31 M per arm in 22 minutes (1.5-1.7 % of history). The late TTFT rise starts at minute 85-86, when the 128-user cap binds, before the first pass. The record's 'not traced request by request' is now traced: 65 zero-hit and 18 partial-hit requests, plus 31 read failures on deleted files.
- Design note §3 eviction class 5 ('queued requests, farthest from admission first') has never been run. return_chance puts every queued turn in one last tier (campaign29/DECISIONS.md:414-419). Class 4 (soft pins) never acted in campaigns 28, 29, 32 or 34. In campaign 26 it bumped 462 staged reads at a cost in SSD traffic, with no TTFT gain shown (campaign26/DECISIONS.md:854-870).
- Paper draft design.tex:184-198 takes queue delays as given and folds them into C. The records show W responds to recompute: campaign 32's queue was 23.32 s against 4.17 s at 36 % against 12 % recompute (campaign32/DECISIONS.md:75,109-113), and campaign 28 shows the chain above. The paper's rules 2 and 4 decided nothing in any saturated run: the price stayed at 5e-5 and there were no bumps (campaigns 28, 29, 32, 34).
- Paper draft design.tex:326-329 ('Recency is a weak proxy for this in agentic workloads') has the same problem as the design note's §3 claim: no TTFT gain from forecast-based ranking was shown in any campaign.
- Paper draft design.tex:107-109 and 128-131: r_i = bL_i/beta, and 'beyond position b/(2 rho beta) reading is cheaper'. The recorded read time includes the read line's queue (parked 17-67 s in campaign 34, 51-66 s in campaign 28). On idle FA3, recompute (about 0.62 s per 20K tokens) is cheaper than the read (0.8-1.1 s), so the cheaper side depends on load (mechanisms_findings.txt:174,230).

## open questions
- Which of the four write-path fixes keeps the first refusals away? This needs runs with fix 2 alone and fix 3 alone (campaign34/DECISIONS.md:171-172).
- There is no measured run-to-run spread at the saturated FA3 setting. At 44 users the spread is 0.10-0.25 s, so the 0.6-0.7 s JIT p50 penalty in campaign 34 rests on one run per arm.
- No setting has both reads and room: at 44 users, 70 GB has reads but only 44K of room, and 100 GB has room but no reads (campaign26/DECISIONS.md:1201-1205). Whether early reads pay when W < r and host room is ample at once has never been tested.
- The -77.7 s hold effect on TraceLab is confounded with the gate, the old oracle's 120 s lead and defect 2. A no-hold arm against a hold arm, both without hints, on the fixed engine would isolate it.
- No just-in-time arm without hints has run on FA3 (the campaign 28/32/34 setting), so whether hints are needed at all on FA3 is open.
- For the cleaner: logging the deleted group keys, or each lookup's first missing page, would confirm that the deleted pages were sessions' heads and separate them from pages never written. The cleaner's share should grow with run length and a smaller volume (one pass about every 10-12 minutes, about 2.5M tokens each); untested.
- Where is the 'Nov 20 trace study' (human turns 76-95 % of resume read time)? It should be saved as a record before the paper cites it.
- Would a cleaner that orders by time to next use, or that spares open sessions, remove the 65 zero-hit and 18 partial-hit losses? Refreshing mtime on reads would be a cheaper test of the same idea; neither is evaluated.
