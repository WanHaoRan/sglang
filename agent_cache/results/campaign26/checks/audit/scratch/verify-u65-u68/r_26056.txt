 - Original HiCache collapses at 44 users / 70 GB and every arm with read pins does not: 5.47 and 6.62 s against 1.89 s or less on the 6,715 shared returning turns; all run pairs have one sign and the smallest gap (3.59 s) is 3x the baseline's own rerun move (1.15 s). It holds on other metrics too: 25.8-29.9 s against 11.5-15.9 s mean turn latency, 6,956-7,541 against 10,037-11,716 turns sent before minute 115.
 - PAPER_PLAN's 79 %: read pins without hints give 72-82 % of the way from original HiCache to exact hints (hint-free 1.61 and 1.89 s; two runs on each side).
 - A p50 forecast acted on as exact is no better than pins alone (JIT p50 1.35 and 1.60 s against hint-free 1.61 and 1.89 s) and is worse than every arm that stages little or nothing: nofc_noend, ec_r13 and JIT exact are 0.64-1.02 s ahead of each of three JIT p50 runs, 3-5x the JIT p50 rerun move (0.18 s). Its mechanisms repeat (staged reads 2,094 and 2,202; launches refused 1,743 and 1,732; mean pins 30.2K and 31.5K tokens), and the new build is not the cause (jit_p50_d_cut - jit_p50_ctl = +0.05 s [-0.04,+0.15] over the first 75 minutes; the second episode is not covered).
 - PAPER_PLAN's caution that hint-free shares are floors because that arm lacks queue-state mechanisms: at 44 users those mechanisms are worth 1.02 s (1.95 -> 0.93 s on 9,703 turns; turns that shrank while queued 288 -> 9), one run each, not separated from one another.
 - The replay's negative predictions held on the GPU: rule 1 ties reading at arrival once recomputation is charged for the queue (ec_r1 0.67 against nofc 0.69 s; 0.86 against 0.88 s); read timing alone does not reach exact hints; most of rule 3's gain is the end signal (reading turns 1,898 without it, 1,581 with it, 1,582 with the three-quantile score).
 - Exact hints under the just-in-time routine are the best arm on TTFT: 0.20 s ahead of the no-hint rival on both turn sets (0.51 against 0.71 s; 0.70 against 0.91 s), same sign in 16 of 16 and 21 of 22 blocks. One run from another date and unit, and not visible in turn latency (-0.05 to -0.12 s, intervals cover zero).
 - Hints reduce SSD read traffic at 44 users, one run each: 562K pages with no hint, 469K with the end signal (-17 %), 437K with three quantiles (-22 %), 388K with exact hints (-31 %). The read thread is 16-21 % busy there, so nothing follows for latency.
 - The measurement plumbing is sound: I reproduced the 6,715 / 11,198 / 9,702 / 6,579 shared sets and every arm's mean on them; decode step, first-turn prefill and SSD ms per page agree within 1-3 % across arms in matched windows; bytes read from the device equal the engine's s2h pages x 6.29 MB (ratio 1.00), so the page cache is not a hidden tier; no client errors in any of the 12 runs.
 - DECISIONS' own reading rule 'a difference under about 0.25 s is not a result on one run each' is consistent with the same-arm pairs (0.25, 0.28 and 1.15 s on the 6,715 turns).

== missing_from_plan
 - A host-memory sweep at fixed users and a non-mirroring write policy: in-progress context peaks at 1.40 M tokens (138 GB) against a 0.712 M pool; a 160 GB pool holds 1.63 M, and GPU plus host without the mirror 1.38 M. Neither the plan nor the queue varies pool size at 44 users.
 - End-to-end metrics as primary results: turn latency, turns and tokens per hour, share of turns within a TTFT target, p99 and maximum, first turns included; and one open-loop arrival run, since the closed loop at 44 sessions slows its own offered load when an arm is slow.
 - A repeat of nofc_noend (the queue repeats nofc only) and a rerun of JIT exact on the current build and unit; a same-arm pair at 128 users; enough runs per arm for a test on run means (4-6).
 - The planned user-count sweep compares the best routine with original HiCache; it needs nofc_noend as the comparison, or it will measure the protection mechanisms again and not the hint.
 - The 2x2 of read start by eviction score (reads at arrival with the return-chance score; rule 1 with the forecast-free order), exact hints under the new rules, and sensitivity to the price and the 30 s horizon on turns the tuning never saw.
 - Each queue-state mechanism alone (queued-turn protection, gate, hold, re-reads), and head-to-head runs against CachedAttention-style look-ahead and Bidaw's hit-potential eviction and read order built in the same engine.
 - Other seeds and traces, a long-context or human-paced workload, a second model or GPU, and a faster SSD: at 2.3-2.5 GB/s the read itself is 0.7 s of a 2.0-2.4 s stall, so a local NVMe shrinks the only quantity read timing can remove.
 - Forecast robustness and incentives: a stale or shifted table, wrong keys, missing end signals, the engine parsing its own reply, and a client that reports short gaps to keep its history in host memory at other tenants' cost.
 - Policy overhead: per-tick cost of rules 1 and 3 with 128 or more plans, and hint bytes per request (3 KB today).
 - SSD write volume and the full-volume regime: write-through wrote about 1.2 TB per 115 min at 44 users (about 15 TB per day on a 2.3 TB volume); starting from an empty volume a 2 h arm stays near half full, below the cleaner's 70 % mark, so the cleaner never runs in these arms.
 - Steady state: every arm is a 2 h cold start with two transient episodes; no run is long enough to average over many episodes or to show recovery after one.
 - More than one GPU or replica: the prefetcher refuses multi-rank setups, and there is no account of a router in front of several engines.
 - Reproducibility: the engine is uncommitted and frozen/ is outside git; four engine freezes on the day of the runs with reviewer-found logic defects and two known ones left; PAPER_PLAN still quotes numbers from deleted runs (the p50's 87 %, the oracle's 0.05-0.14 s spread); GPU units are unrecorded for campaigns 19-21.
 - Limits of this review (not a plan item): I ran only CPU scripts on the 12 finished runs (/home/wanhr/sglang/agent_cache/results/campaign26/checks/verify/reviewer/, review1-8.py with .out files); I did not read the run in progress, could not check rules 2 and 4 (no runs), could not separate GPU-unit from run-to-run effects, and took campaign 18's x70 figures and the AUC values from the project's notes and PAPER_PLAN without recomputing them.

== defensible_headline
 On one H200 with 44 concurrent agent sessions, a 70 GB host pool and tool gaps stretched x10 (one trace, one seed, 2 h runs), SGLang's three-tier HiCache spends its load episodes recomputing history it had already read back: mean TTFT of returning turns 5.5 and 6.6 s in two runs, 25.8-29.9 s per turn end to end, 7,541 and 6,956 turns served. Protecting what queued turns need (read pins, queued-turn eviction protection, an arrival gate and hold, re-reads; no gap forecast and no end signal) brings this to 0.71 s (7.7-9.3x lower), 11.6 s per turn and 11,680 turns (+55 to +68 %), 96 % of the way to exact next-arrival hints (0.51 s). Acting on a p50 forecast as if it were exact is worse than not forecasting (1.35-1.60 s in three runs, the new build included). Three-quantile forecasts under rules 1 and 3 add 0.05-0.15 s of TTFT and 22 % fewer SSD pages over that rival in one run each: a margin not yet separated from the 0.25-0.28 s that reruns of one arm moved, and not visible in turn latency or throughput.
