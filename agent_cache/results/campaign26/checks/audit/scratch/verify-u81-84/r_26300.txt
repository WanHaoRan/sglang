Half right: the mechanism is as you say, but a larger pool does not give *more* gain, because it also removes most of the reads there are to start early.

- **Short queue, read at arrival:** outside the load episodes a turn that has to read from SSD has a median TTFT of 1.18 s with no hints, against 0.34 s for a turn that reads nothing. So the read costs about 1 s when nothing else is in the way.
- **Short queue, early read timed right:** with exact hints the median reading turn is at 0.34 s, the same as a turn that read nothing. So starting the load early does remove the cost.
- **But few turns read:** only 5 to 10 % of turns read there, so the gain is about 0.06 s per turn on average. It shows in the tail of TTFT, not in the mean or in throughput.
- **A larger pool cuts the reads:** live sessions hold a median of 1,176K tokens (1,443K at most).
  - A 70 GB pool holds 712K, with 44K free beyond the GPU's copy.
  - A 100 GB pool holds 1,017K, with 349K free. About a third as much history sits on SSD, and there is room to start all of those reads early.
  - A 160 GB pool holds 1,628K, more than the sessions ever hold, so nothing is on SSD and there is nothing to load early.

So more room makes early loading safe, but the total gain is roughly the number of reads times that 1 s, and the number of reads falls as the pool grows. The setting where early loading should pay most is one with many reads, a short queue and room all at once: x70 gaps at 128 users and 160 GB. That one is not queued.
