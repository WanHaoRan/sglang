## What rule 1 decides

A session's history is on SSD only and its turn has not arrived. Rule 1 picks when to start reading it back: now, at some later time σ, or only when the turn arrives ([slide 17](https://claude.ai/artifact/F9RQpNBrtGdEdsn52ctZg2#slide-17)).

- **Landing late** stalls the turn, at 1 second of TTFT per second.
- **Landing early** pins L tokens of host memory until admission, at λL per second (λ is the rent).
- **The safety net:** if the turn arrives before σ, its arrival starts the read. So the stall never exceeds the read time r = bL/β, and a bad hint can cost room but never extra stall.

The session minimizes J(σ) = E[stall] + λL · E[seconds pinned], given it has not returned yet.

## The derivation

Delaying the start by a moment does two things:

- It adds stall if the turn arrives within the next r seconds: probability Pr(σ < A ≤ σ + r).
- It saves rent if the turn arrives later than that: λL · Pr(A > σ + r).

Setting the two equal and dividing by Pr(A > σ) gives the rule ([slide 18](https://claude.ai/artifact/F9RQpNBrtGdEdsn52ctZg2#slide-18)):

**Start once F(r | t) ≥ q, with q = λL / (1 + λL).**

F(r | t) is the chance the turn arrives within one read time, given how long the session has been idle. At λ = 5 × 10⁻⁵:

| History | Read time r | q |
|---|---|---|
| 8K | 0.38 s | 0.29 |
| 32K | 1.53 s | 0.62 |
| 64K | 3.05 s | 0.77 |
| 128K | 6.11 s | 0.87 |

Those two lines are one algebra step that the slide skips: divide the derivative by the chance that the turn has not arrived yet. (The symbol is σ, the read's start time, not δ.)

**Start from the derivative**

```
dJ/dσ = P1 − λL · P2

P1 = Pr(σ < A ≤ σ + r)   the turn arrives during the read   → waiting adds stall
P2 = Pr(A > σ + r)       the turn arrives after the read    → waiting saves rent
```

**The two probabilities add up to "not arrived by σ"**

If the turn has not arrived by σ, it either arrives within the next r seconds or later than that. So:

```
P1 + P2 = Pr(A > σ)
```

**F is P1 as a share of that**

F(r | σ) is the chance the turn arrives within one read time, given it has not arrived by σ:

```
F = P1 / (P1 + P2)      so   P1 = Pr(A > σ) · F
                             P2 = Pr(A > σ) · (1 − F)
```

**Substitute and factor**

```
dJ/dσ = Pr(A > σ) · [ F − λL · (1 − F) ]
      = Pr(A > σ) · [ F · (1 + λL) − λL ]
      = Pr(A > σ) · (1 + λL) · [ F − λL / (1 + λL) ]
      = (a positive number) · [ F(r | σ) − q ]
```

That is what "∝ F(r | σ) − q" means: the derivative has the same sign as F − q. The fraction λL / (1 + λL) falls out of the factoring, and the slide names it q.

**Reading it**

- **F < q:** the derivative is negative, so starting later lowers the cost. Keep waiting.
- **F > q:** the derivative is positive, so starting later raises the cost. Start now.
- **F = q:** the minimum, which gives "start once F(r | t) ≥ q".

**Why q has that form**

q is the break-even point between the two costs of waiting one more moment:

- With chance F the turn comes during the read, and the delay costs 1 (a second of stall per second).
- With chance 1 − F it comes later, and the delay saves λL (a second of rent on L tokens).

Setting F · 1 = (1 − F) · λL and solving gives F = λL / (1 + λL). So q is the cost of a second early divided by the cost of a second early plus a second late.

**With numbers**

For a 64K history at λ = 5 × 10⁻⁵, λL = 3.28: a second early costs 3.28 times a second late. Then q = 3.28 / 4.28 = 0.77. At F = 0.77 the stall side is 0.77 × 1 = 0.77 and the rent side is 0.23 × 3.28 = 0.75, so the two balance. The read should start only once the turn is 77 % likely to arrive within the next 3 seconds.

The slide would be easier to follow with the line Pr(A > σ) = P1 + P2 shown between the derivative and the "∝" step.

## Where the score comes from

The slide states the rule and cites Cao et al.; the derivation is short. Suppose staged read j is bumped at time t and read again as soon as possible:

```
pages are back at t + r_j at the earliest
turn j stalls only if it arrives before then:     chance = F_j(r_j | t)
expected stall = area under F_j(x | t), 0 to r_j  ≈ ½ · r_j · F_j(r_j | t)
tokens freed   = L_j = (β / b) · r_j

expected stall per token freed ≈ (b / 2β) · F_j(r_j | t) = 23 µs × F_j
```

The read time cancels: a larger staged read frees more room but takes that much longer to bring back. So ordering by F alone is ordering by expected stall per token of room gained.

## Why the arrived turn always outranks a staged read

Per token of room:

```
refuse the arrived read:  1/ρ ≈ 110 µs of GPU prefill, and every turn queued behind it waits too
bump a staged read:       at most b/β = 47 µs of stall, for one turn, with the GPU free for others
```

Even a staged read whose turn returns at once is cheaper to bump than an arrived read is to refuse. That is Cao et al.'s "do no harm": never drop data needed now to keep data needed later. F only orders the staged reads among themselves.

What is c_i in rule 3?

`c_i` is what it costs, in seconds of TTFT per token, to get history i back after evicting it. It takes one of two values:

```
c_i = b/β ≈ 47 µs per token    the tokens are on SSD: read them back
c_i = 1/ρ ≈ 110 µs per token   the SSD lacks them: recompute them on the GPU
```

A history with both kinds of pages gets the per-token average.

**What it does in the score.** `F_i(H | t) · c_i` is the expected time lost per token of room freed: the chance the history is needed, times what each token costs to restore. It is per token because eviction frees tokens, and it puts the score in the same units as the price.

**When it changes the order.** Only when some history has pages missing from SSD.
- For histories wholly on SSD, `c_i` is the same 47 µs for all, so it drops out and the order is by F alone.
- Missing pages cost 2.4 times as much, so such a history is kept longer. A history on SSD with F = 0.9 scores 42 µs; one not on SSD with F = 0.5 scores 55 µs and stays.

**Rule 2 has no `c`** for the same reason: every staged read came from SSD.

**In the build, `c_i` is not there.** The eviction policy ranks by F alone (`python/sglang/srt/mem_cache/next_use_eviction.py:258`), which assumes every history is on SSD. With write-through that is the normal case.

**One caveat for the paper.** The two values are not the same kind of cost. The 47 µs is a stall for one turn, and only if the read is not back in time. The 110 µs is GPU time, which also delays every turn queued behind it; campaign 23 puts that at 6–8 times the prefill itself. So `c_i` for missing pages is understated once the queue is counted.

Changing λ does not change which session is ahead of which. It changes how much room all sessions ask for in total, and that total is the one thing that has to match the room.

## Where λ enters a decision

λ is the rent on host room, in seconds of TTFT per token-second. At 5 × 10⁻⁵, holding 20K tokens for one second costs as much as one second of stall.

- **Rule 1** uses it in the threshold q = λL / (1 + λL). A higher λ raises q, so the read starts later.
- **Rule 3's keep test** on the slide uses the same q. The build uses rule 3 only as a ranking, so in the build λ acts through rule 1 alone.
- **The rankings** (rule 2, rule 3's eviction order) do not use λ at all.

I computed one 64K read on the deck's sharp 20 s key at different prices:

| λ | q | Read starts | Expected stall | Expected time pinned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | at once | 0 s | 17.1 s |
| 5 × 10⁻⁶ | 0.25 | 15.7 s into the gap | 0.27 s | 1.66 s |
| 5 × 10⁻⁵ | 0.77 | 19.0 s | 1.81 s | 0.20 s |
| 5 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.97 | in effect at arrival | 3.04 s | 0 s |

The price slides each session along its own trade between stall and room.

## Why one shared λ, and why it must move

Each session decides alone, and λ is the only thing through which it sees the others. Because it is the same for all, it leaves their order alone and moves the total. In an offline check on 1,981 real turns (room not modelled), going from 5 × 10⁻⁶ to 2 × 10⁻⁵ cuts the average tokens pinned from 22K to 8K and raises total stall from 857 to 1,561 s.

The right total depends on the room, and the room is not fixed:

- **Across settings:** λ is about 0 at 160 GB, where room is plentiful, and 5 × 10⁻⁵ at 70 GB.
- **Across room sizes in the replay:** the best fixed λ is 1 × 10⁻⁴ for a 70K room, 5 × 10⁻⁵ at 90K and 2 × 10⁻⁵ at 110K.
- **Within a run:** the room is contested in load episodes and idle between them.

With too low a price everyone reads early, the room fills, and arrived turns are refused, which is JIT p50's failure. With too high a price the room sits idle while turns stall. Rule 4 searches for the price in between: refused room pushes λ up, idle room pulls it down.

This is the outer loop that replaces solving the optimization. Rules 1 and 3 are each session's best answer to a price, and rule 4 finds the price at which those answers fit the room. Without it, λ is a constant tuned by hand for one room.

## What the build has found so far

- **The deck's formula does not work as stated.** Replayed on a real run's signals, it drives λ to its floor within a minute. The room is idle on average (75K tokens) and refused only in bursts, so "refused minus idle" is positive in only 9 % of intervals.
- **The build's second form prices by harm:** the recomputation that staged pins left to arrived turns, per token-second pinned. On the same run it stays near 3 × 10⁻⁵ without being told the calibrated 5 × 10⁻⁵.
- **The first live test is running now.** `ec_r134` is 57 minutes in and ends about 12:40 pm ET; it has no TTFT yet.
  - The price started at 5 × 10⁻⁵ and has swung between 2 × 10⁻⁶ and 5.7 × 10⁻⁵, with a median of 2.5 × 10⁻⁵.
  - Staged pins rose to 11–16K tokens in the last 20 minutes, against 1–4K at the fixed price.
  - As the first load episode began, 332 s of harm was charged in 7 minutes. The fixed-price run had 457 s in two hours.
  - This may be the price lagging the load, but the run is half done.
- **The real test of rule 4** is `ec_r134_c128`, about 2:40 to 4:45 pm ET today: whether the same code finds a price near zero at 160 GB.

What is tokens refused room?

=== line 1178 @ 2026-11-12T16:43:57.369Z ASSISTANT len=1573 ===
It is the unmet demand for room: tokens of history that a read tried to bring from SSD into host memory and could not, because the room was full when the read launched. Rule 4 sums it over each 5 s interval.

Three things count toward it in the build:

1. **A read refused whole.** No room is found even after evicting idle history, so the read's full size counts.
2. **A read cut short.** Only a prefix fits, so the part cut off counts.
3. **With soft pins, a bumped staged read.** Its tokens count, as slide 22 asks.

Early reads and reads of arrived turns both count. The slide also lists "histories pushed out while their score beat the price"; the build does not count those.

**What happens to those tokens.** An arrived turn retries the lookup while it waits, up to three lookups in all. Whatever it still lacks at admission is recomputed on the GPU at about 110 µs per token. A refused early read is left to the turn's arrival.

**Two intervals from the run in progress** (R = 43,968 tokens):

```
refused 75,712   idle 14,726   g = (75,712 − 14,726) / 43,968 = +1.39   price goes up
refused  5,440   idle 28,605   g = ( 5,440 − 28,605) / 43,968 = −0.53   price goes down
```

**Do not confuse it with "launches refused room"** in the run tables (1,732–1,743 for JIT p50, 395 under rule 1). Those count reads; rule 4 counts their tokens.

**The two terms are not the same kind of quantity.** Refused is a flow (tokens per 5 s) and idle is a stock (tokens of free room, averaged over the interval). That mismatch is part of why the deck's formula sinks to its floor.
