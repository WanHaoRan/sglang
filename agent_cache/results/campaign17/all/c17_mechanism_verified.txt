=== NARRATIVE
Campaign 17 ran campaign 14's workload with gaps x10 on one frozen engine. The original HiCache baseline (write-through, wait_complete, paced retries of 8 attempts at an 8-pass interval, the #39283 re-query at admission, LRU eviction) ran against campaign 16's _oracle_v2_nu. On the 20,293 shared turns the oracle arm's mean TTFT is 11.25 s against 15.77 s: a paired -4.52 s [-5.51, -3.56], p99 27.6 vs 116.4 s, and recomputed history of 36 vs 787 tokens per returning turn (all/c17_summary.txt). That gain is a single storm that the baseline had and the oracle arm did not. Split by the baseline's send time (reconcile/out/checks2.txt, which reproduces key_stats/out_main.txt):
- Before 76 min: -0.08 s per pair over 9,252 pairs. Both arms averaged 17.3 s at 70-75 min.
- 76-115 min: the oracle arm is 70.1 s faster per pair over 1,758 pairs. That is -6.07 s of the overall mean, or 134 % of the gain.
- After 115 min: the oracle arm is 3.46 s slower per pair (+1.58 s of the mean).

All 1,569 baseline turns above 60 s were sent at 76.4-114.5 min; the oracle arm's slowest turn took 32.8 s (reconcile/out/misc.txt). The after-storm penalty comes from admission control, not slower service. The storm drove the baseline's user cap from 128 (82 min) down to 76 (116 min), with up to 228 users waiting. 287 of its users waited more than 60 s to start, and admission waits total 170.3 vs 28.1 user-hours. The baseline therefore served its late turns behind short queues, while the oracle arm had served the same turns a median 1,699 s earlier, during its own congested phase at 17-22 s TTFT. Measured from arrival to finish, users finished 852 s sooner on average in the oracle arm (key_stats/out_users.txt). Outside the storm both arms hit the same GPU limit: 2.35 vs 2.36 turns/s in congested bins (key_stats/out_checks.txt).

The storm was campaign 11's failure, not campaign 14's. Of the baseline's 15.36M recomputed history tokens, 95.5 % are in turns sent at 76-115 min. 13.94M of them (90.7 %) belong to 839 turns whose history was on GPU or host memory when they arrived and was evicted while they waited in the queue, after their 8-attempt lookup budget was spent. Lost-after-landing accounts for only 45 turns and 0.78M tokens (5 %). The budget is used up by how the retry code works, not by data missing from the SSD:
- A returning turn's arrival lookup covers only its new messages. Those were never prefilled, so they are never on the SSD.
- If that span is 256 tokens or more, the frozen prefetch_from_storage issues the lookup, which misses, and the request re-polls every 8 scheduler passes until the cap. There were 63,221 paced lookups and none hit 256 or more tokens. There were 7,242 cap lines, 6,910 of them on returning turns.
- After the cap, _prefetch_after_device_hit_loss refuses the re-query.
- If the span is under 256 tokens, prefetch_from_storage returns before arming a retry, so the budget stays whole.

The run contains its own control. Among turns sent at 4,500-7,200 s, at the same queue wait (p50 83.1 vs 82.5 s):
- The 1,053 turns with a short tail lost their history while queued 792 times. All 792 were re-read from the SSD by the re-query.
- The 1,329 turns with a long tail lost it 840 times and recomputed 839 of those (13.94M tokens).

These counts are in reconcile/out/natural_exp.txt, and they reproduce recompute-attribution's T4. The re-query's reads landed a median 0.96 s before admission, and none was lost after landing.

Timeline of the storm:
- About 50 min: the GPU limit is reached. Half of the returning turns spend their budgets, harmlessly so far: 6 of 1,676 recomputed at 50-72 min (gpu-and-queue).
- 71-72 min: host evictions jump 3-6x in both arms; the cause is not determined. Queued contexts start being evicted. In the baseline these were only the contexts of turns that issued no lookup (75 of 352 at 70-76 min), and all were restored.
- From 76 min: the polling turns lose their history too (838 of 974 sent at 76-115 min). Recompute climbs from 1.2K to 6.7K tok/s within a minute. Decode falls to 125-152 tok/s in the 10-min bins at 80-110 min (gpu-and-queue/report.txt). Queue waits reach about 90 s, and 97 % of returning turns lose history while queued (reconcile/out/loss_bins.txt).
- 115-116 min: the storm ends, after the cap has cut live users from 128 to about 90.

At x70 the mix was the reverse: campaign 14's recompute was 59 % lost-after-landing and 32 % spent budgets.

The oracle arm stayed out of the storm because every one of its queued losses had a way back, not because its hint-driven levers made service faster.
- At the onset (70-75 min), with the same queue (p50 16-17 s) and the same TTFT (17.3 s), it lost queued contexts about as often as the baseline: 6.7 % vs 7.8 % of returning turns (loss_bins.txt).
- But no request had spent its lookup budget, because poll interval 0 means no paced lookups.
- Case-2 refetch re-read the lost spans. 246 of its 247 queue-time losses over the run were restored: 244 by refetch and 2 by the re-query.
- Pins held landed reads: lost-after-landing 3 turns vs 45.

DECISIONS.md expected the oracle to be mostly idle at x10; the hint-driven levers were busy instead.
- Next-use eviction, together with staging allocations and pins holding 15-17 % of host memory, shifted evictions onto contexts between turns. At 70-75 min, 28.7 % of returning turns had history evicted before arrival, against 4.4 % in the baseline; over the run, 2,686 vs 433.
- Case-1 staging read 2,636 of those back. Staged reads carried 89.4 % of the SSD-credited tokens, and none landed late (oracle_eval.csv late = 0).
- SSD reads doubled: 47.8M vs 22.3M tokens, 4.70 vs 2.19 TB (checks2.txt).
- The SSD ran at 97.5-98 % utilisation at 80-100 min. It was at least 90 % busy in 58 % of the 60-120 min samples, against 8 % for the baseline (reconcile/out/iostat.txt).
- Host memory served less history than under LRU: 49 % vs 52 %. At x70, next-use had raised that share from 16 % to 29 %.

The only window where the levers were active and the baseline was not storming is 70-76 min, and TTFT there is equal.

How much of the -4.52 s the hint-free change (poll interval 0) explains can only be bounded by inference. It could be all of it. The gain is the storm, 90.7 % of the baseline's recompute went through budgets spent by paced polls, and every loss that kept its budget was restored at the same queue wait. But none of the four analyses reported that the polls also protected history while requests queued. In the baseline, turns with a long new tail almost never lost their history while queued outside the storm, while short-tail turns did:
- 50-76 min: 0 of 2,126 long-tail turns vs 75 of 1,673 short-tail turns.
- After 115 min: 2 of 4,992 vs 32 of 4,004.

The two groups had the same gap (7.0-7.1 s), queue wait and prompt size. In the oracle arm, where nothing polls, both groups lost history at the same rate: 26 of 343 vs 19 of 259 at 70-76 min (reconcile/out/class_by_arm.txt). The mechanism is not established. Each poll re-matches the prefix, which refreshes the LRU stamps along its path. But the protection also held for turns admitted 10-30 s after their last poll: 0 of 531, against 75 of 912 (reconcile/out/untouched.txt).

So a baseline with polls off would lose more queued contexts and would have to re-read each one at admission. The storm's 13.94M recomputed tokens alone come to about 1.37 TB. Those reads would run under LRU and without pins, where each read's host allocation evicts more queued contexts. Whether that loop stays bounded is exactly what DECISIONS.md's unrun third arm (three_tier_wc with poll interval 0) would show. The evidence from within the run favours a large share for the retry change, but it cannot rule out a different storm.

Each arm ran once. The gain is a single 39-min episode shared by 145 users, so intervals from resampling users are optimistic, and two runs of identical code differed by 0.98 s in smoke C. Given that the baseline storms, the direction of the gain is well supported: the mechanism is measured turn by turn, and both earlier x10 baselines with paced retries (campaigns 11 and 12, with 2,162 and 1,541 cap lines) collapsed the same way. Its size is not supported, because it depends on when and for how long a storm runs. The oracle arm's clean run also sat close to its limits, with exact hints:
- the SSD at 98 % for about 20 min;
- the pin budget full 143 times;
- the longest queue wait at 32.6 s, against the 60-s overdue threshold.

Once definitions are aligned, the four analyses' counts agree. The one factual error is gpu-and-queue's claim that the storm turns that still had their budget had history only on the SSD at arrival. In fact 851 of the 852 re-queried turns had a short arrival span and no SSD query before the re-query. Their shrink line's lookup_match also equals their arrival match (reconcile/out/check_requery.txt).

Not determined:
- what raised host evictions at 71 min in both arms;
- how the polls protect queued history;
- how the oracle arm's eviction shift splits between next-use ordering, staging and pins;
- run-to-run spread.

=== DISAGREEMENTS
 - gpu-and-queue vs recompute-attribution, on the storm turns that still had their budget. gpu-and-queue says the 774 re-queried turns 'already had their history only on the SSD when they arrived' (arrival lookup hit 774 of 774, tokens_req p50 16,448). That is wrong. 851 of the 852 re-queried returning turns had an arrival span under 256 tokens and no SSD query before the re-query. In all 852, the shrink line's lookup_match equals the device+host match at arrival. Their first s2h_query came a median 88.9 s after entry, at the re-query itself (reconcile/out/check_requery.txt). For example, c354-t28 had arrival prefetch_start tokens=123; after 100 s its prefix shrank from 20,864 to 2,624, and then an 18,240-token read followed. gpu-and-queue took the re-query's lookup for the arrival lookup. recompute-attribution is right: both groups were resident at arrival and lost their history while queued, and only the budget differed.
 - gpu-and-queue says the first discrete change, at 71-72 min, was common to both arms: short-gap returns began finding their history only on the SSD. The timing is common, but where history was lost is not. At 70-75 min, with equal queue and TTFT, the baseline's losses were mostly while queued (7.8 %, against 4.4 % before arrival). 54 of its 83 SSD-served turns at 72-76 min were re-queries (gpu-and-queue's own table D). The oracle arm's losses were before arrival (28.7 %, against 6.7 % while queued) (reconcile/out/loss_bins.txt). Likewise, recompute-attribution's 'ran identically until 75-76 min' holds for TTFT and throughput, not for the cache.
 - oracle-activity vs recompute-attribution, on next-use eviction. oracle-activity says next-use 'kept queued requests' contexts' (evicted while queued 1,687 -> 187); recompute-attribution says the roughly 7x cannot be separated from the storm. recompute-attribution is right. At equal load (70-75 min), queued-loss rates were 7.8 % in the baseline and 6.7 % in the oracle arm. The gap opened only after the baseline's queue waits passed 30 s. The shift of losses to before arrival (4.4 % -> 28.7 %) is real.
 - oracle-activity says turning paced retries off only hurt the baseline, and recompute-attribution says poll interval 0 'removes' 90.7 % of the recompute. The first is overstated and the second is an inference, not a measurement. The polls protected polling turns from eviction while queued: 0 of 2,126 lost against 75 of 1,673 at 50-76 min. In the oracle arm, which has no polls, the rates were 7.6 % vs 7.3 % (reconcile/out/class_by_arm.txt). Without polls the baseline would therefore have more SSD re-reads at admission, and the outcome of that third arm is unknown.
 - The count variants are definitional, not conflicts. Budget-mechanism turns:
- 839 causal (recompute-attribution; I reproduced it);
- 883 flagged by the cap line and recomputing more than 1 page (oracle-activity; for 44 of them the loss predated arrival);
- 841 admitted at 76-116 min (gpu-and-queue);
- 891 cap-flagged storm-sent turns with any recompute (statistics).
All four put the tokens at about 13.9M. Queue-time losses: 1,691 and 247 count any arrival-resident loss over 1 page (I reproduced both); 1,687 and 187 require the full history resident at arrival. Oracle-arm lost after landing: 2 under the per-read rule, 3 under the summed rule.
 - The storm edges agree within a minute: 76-115 min by send time and recompute over 1,000 tokens per turn (statistics); 77-114 min by 1-min decode at or below 250 tok/s (gpu-and-queue); a divergence at 76-77 min (recompute-attribution). The brief's '60-120 min' comes from 30-min bins.
 - statistics says the oracle arm 'started everyone on arrival'. That holds only until about 100 min: its cap then fell to 90 by 138 min, and 173 users waited more than 60 s to start (max 900 s), against 287 in the baseline (max 2,609 s) (reconcile/out/misc.txt). The admission-control explanation of the after-storm penalty still stands.
 - DECISIONS.md's expectations vs the data. It expected the baseline to recompute once queue waits passed about 10 s; budget-spent turns waited about 10 s at 50-72 min and only 6 of 1,676 recomputed (gpu-and-queue). Recompute began at 76 min, once the polling turns also started losing their history. It expected the oracle to be mostly idle; instead it staged 2,636 spans and doubled SSD reads.
