## Full texts opened
lenstra-shmoys "Elements of Scheduling", arXiv:2001.06005 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06005, PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.06005), text kept in pdf_txt/:
  - ch. 3 intro: "According to Jackson's Earliest Due Date (EDD) rule, if there is any way to complete all of the jobs on time, then it can be accomplished by performing the jobs in order of nondecreasing due dates. Furthermore, such an EDD order minimizes maximum lateness, whether or not it meets all of the due dates."
  - Theorem 3.1 [Jackson's EDD rule]: "Any EDD schedule is optimal for the problem 1||Lmax." Corollary 3.2: "For any instance of 1||Lmax, all due dates can be met if and only if they are met in any EDD schedule."
  - "the unrestricted problem 1|r_j|Lmax is strongly NP-hard"; "nonuniform release dates are innocuous when preemption is permitted, but calamatous when it is not."
  - Theorem 3.5: "The preemptive EDD rule solves the problem 1|pmtn, r_j|Lmax, with L*max = max_{S} r(S) + p(S) - d(S)." Notes 3.2: "Horn [1974] observed that 1|pmtn, r_j|Lmax and 1|r_j, p_j = 1|Lmax are solved by the preemptive EDD rule."
  - "the preemptive EDD rule creates preemptions only at release dates"; "the preemptive EDD rule is on-line ... without requiring any knowledge of their processing times and of the release dates of future jobs."
  - Theorem 4.1: "A sequence is optimal for 1||sum w_j C_j if and only if it places the jobs in order of nonincreasing ratios w_j/p_j." Notes 4.1: "The ratio rule is, of course, due to W. E. Smith (1956)."
  - ch. 1: "Minimization of total tardiness sum T_j and total weighted tardiness sum w_j T_j ... Note that these criteria reduce to sum C_j and sum w_j C_j respectively if d_j = 0 for all j."
  - ch. 8: "1||sum T_j and 1|pmtn|sum T_j are NP-hard ... 1||sum w_j T_j and 1|pmtn|sum w_j T_j are NP-hard in the strong sense".
  - bibliography: "J.R. Jackson (1955). Scheduling a production line to minimize maximum tardiness, Research Report 43, Management Science Research Project, University of California, Los Angeles."; "W.A. Horn (1974).
