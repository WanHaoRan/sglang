G: gap · A: arrival time · W: queue wait · T: time of next use on the GPU · r: read time, including the wait in the SSD’s line · (z)⁺ = max(z, 0)

The model has one clock. For each session, the question behind every decision is when its KV is next needed on the GPU. Call that time T. It is the sum of two parts that different parties know. The first is the arrival of the next request, A: the end of the current turn plus the gap G. Before it happens, only the agent can forecast it, because the agent knows which tool it is about to run; it sends that forecast to the engine as a hint, which is a distribution, not a number. The second part is the queue wait W: once the request has arrived, other turns may go first, and the engine can estimate how long from the turns ahead of it and the rate at which it admits them. The KV has to be on the GPU at T. A read from the SSD takes r seconds, counting its wait in the SSD’s line, so it has to start by T minus r. One quantity will come back on many slides: x, the positive part of r minus W. It is the part of a read that the queue does not hide. If the queue wait is longer than the read, a read started at arrival lands in time and costs the turn nothing. Earlier versions of this design took the queue wait as a constant; here it is part of the model, and it changes the answers.
