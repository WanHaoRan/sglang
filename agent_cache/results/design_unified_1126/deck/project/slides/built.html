Twelve environment switches and one eviction setting on the SGLang fork; the engine copy of each arm is frozen and named in the campaign’s records. Not built: reads held against writes by free room, and the pass started exactly when its copy’s remainder equals its compute.

This is what the engine now has. Every rule sits behind its own switch, and every switch is off by default, so an arm with all of them off is the current design. Fetch has five entries. On the top tier, the copy from host memory starts when the batch is built and the running turns keep decoding until it has landed. On the SSD tier, a held read starts at a quantile of the measured error of the admission estimate instead of a fixed two seconds; before arrival, the start rule weighs the stall by c and counts the SSD’s line in the read time; and a lookup that finds nothing no longer costs the turn one of its three lookups. Read or recompute by price, and the rescue of a GPU starved by the SSD, are built and left off, because the simulator shows a loss. Retain has two. On the SSD, pages in use are stamped recent, a finished session’s own pages go first, and the cleaner checks a page’s time again before deleting it. And a session counts as over only when its hint says so, which removes the 410 false ends per run; its last turn is then not written. Preempt: among queued turns the latest arrival gives up its history first, where the engine used to take the turn nearest admission. Price: mu, c, lambda and kappa are measured from the queues, logged every ten seconds, and used in place of the fixed values. Two things guard the build. With every switch off, a side-by-side replay of 240 random scenarios produces the same log lines and the same cache calls as the code before the change, so off means unchanged. And each half of the build was reviewed by three reviewers, for logic, for the off-by-default contract and for tests, and then fixed; the six reviews found fifteen real defects, for example that the price log changed which page was evicted, and each was fixed with a test before any GPU hour was spent. Preempt on the GPU's room is built too: when the turn at the head of the queue does not fit the free room, a later turn that fits is admitted behind it, up to eight times before the head is waited for again; measured on top of everything else in campaign 44, it ties on the mean and trades a lower median for a heavier tail, so it stays off. A second review covered the scheduler side, the hold and this admission rule: it found two more defects, a memory check that did not count a held batch and a size test that could hold a batch with a small copy, and neither touches what the arms measure. Not built: holding reads against writes by free room, and starting the prefill pass exactly when the rest of its copy equals its own compute, which the proposition says gains about 26 milliseconds per held turn.
