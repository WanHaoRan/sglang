This slide collects what is proved, each with the assumption it needs and the classic result it rests on. First, a read after arrival lands best at the q-quantile of the admission time: that is the newsvendor argument, and it needs only that the cost is linear in stall and in rent. Second, before arrival, starting the read the first time the chance of return within the visible read time reaches q is the best rule at every price exactly when that chance never falls as the session stays idle. Third, an exact identity: the gain of keeping a copy over fetching it back is never negative, so a read before arrival pays only where keeping would have paid. Fourth, the holding rule: keep while the index is above the price, and the first idle age at which it is not is the best moment to drop; the index is the Gittins index of a job of unknown size. Fifth, with exact hints the index order is Belady’s rule, and with one falling-rate law for all sessions it is least recently used, in both cases for equal sizes and restore costs. Sixth, at a steady device price a write is never better early than late, by an interchange argument; it is due when the copy would be given up. Seventh, on the device, serving by earliest due time meets every due time if any order does, within one page group because transfers switch only at page groups. Eighth and ninth, the queue: for every policy that keeps the GPU pool full in a closed loop, the mean TTFT is N minus B times the GPU-seconds per request, minus the pause; so no policy gets below that expression at the smallest possible GPU time. What is not proved is that ranking many sessions by the index is the best use of a hard capacity. It is a heuristic there, and a counterexample with one slot and two sessions shows it is not always optimal; in simulation it is 28 percent above the priced bound with 32 sessions, 3.5 percent with 128 and within 1 percent with 512, while least recently used stays about 30 percent above. Each result was derived, checked numerically on random cases, and then derived again by a referee: of 39 results, 33 stand as stated, 5 gained a condition, such as a finite mean gap for the cost of an early read, and one, the many-session limit, is left open. Several claims of the first draft did not survive and were corrected. The final list is 21 theorems and one proposition, written out with proofs in the design folder. The last row was added after the referee and is the one result of the list that needs no price at all. A prefill pass that needs p seconds of compute, started beside a copy of its history that ends at r, finishes at the larger of its start plus p and r, because the layers land and are used in order. So the start r minus p, or at once if p is longer, finishes as early as starting at once and never makes the GPU wait: any earlier start wastes GPU time and any later one delays the turn.
