The four actions · Preempt

Preempt: the lowest index gives way

Host memory

A read needs room and none is free. They yield in this order: sessions that are over; idle sessions, by index; reads staged for sessions not yet back; queued turns, latest arrival first. Never the head of the queue, a page with no SSD copy, or a read in flight.

SSD

Reads go by due time: if any order lands them all in time, this one does. A late write holds room worth L · (λ − ν) a second; it goes before a late read of L′ tokens when (λ − ν) · L′ > c. Today’s fixed hold is one point of that rule.

GPU

A turn whose read has not landed is passed over, so the GPU never waits for the SSD while ready work exists. That is what makes c small under load. If nothing may yield room, a read waits: a refusal becomes a recompute.

What the order guarantees

Taking holders in increasing index frees room at the least priced cost. Under a hard limit on room it is exactly optimal for exact hints with equal sizes (Belady) and for one heavy-tailed hint law (LRU); otherwise its gap to the priced floor shrinks as sessions grow: 6 % at 128 sessions, 2 to 3 % from 512.

ν: holding index · λ: price of a host token-second · c: weight of a second of stall · L, L′: tokens of the page written, of the read. Once some reads must be late no simple order is best for the sum of stalls; the choice then only decides who waits.

Retain says what to keep when there is time to decide. Preempt says who gives way when a resource is full right now, and it uses the same index. Host memory first. A read needs room and none is free. The holders yield in increasing index: sessions that are over; idle sessions by their index; reads that were staged for sessions not yet back; and then queued turns, the latest arrival first. Three things never yield: the turn at the head of the queue, whose re-read could not land in time; a page with no SSD copy; and a read in flight. On the SSD, reads are served by due time, because if any order lands them all in time, this one does. A write that is late holds host room worth L times lambda minus nu per second, and it goes before a late read exactly when that rate, on the tokens the read needs, exceeds c; the engine’s fixed rule, where timed reads wait while pending writes hold 50 thousand tokens, is one point of this comparison. On the GPU, a turn whose read has not landed is passed over, so the GPU never waits for the SSD while ready work exists; that is what makes c small under load.
