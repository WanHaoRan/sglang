How the design got here, in eight iterations. Stock HiCache keeps a host copy of every page, writes through to the SSD, evicts the least recently used and reads on arrival; on this workload it is at 31.9 seconds, and reads that had landed were evicted before use. The oracles used exact gaps: pins on landed reads, reads served by due time, and eviction by next use took an earlier workload from 18.5 to 5.7 seconds. The hint table made the hint realistic, the tenth, fiftieth and ninetieth percentile per kind of tool call, with reads timed to land just before use; the median alone kept 87 percent of the gain at light load. The no-hint designs use only the queue: they protect queued turns and time reads by queue position, and get 96 percent of the way to exact hints. The four rules, the current design, use the whole distribution and a price on host memory; they reach 10.96 seconds at saturation and tie with the no-hint design. The write-path fixes ended a loop of refused reads and rewrites, worth 5.8 to 9.5 seconds. The note of 23 November put everything on one clock but was never built. This design puts the queue, the hint’s shape and the write inside the four actions, and adds a floor and a list of the levers left. Across the iterations three things carried the gain: pins on landed reads, protection of queued turns, and a write path that does not back up. Three things did not: reads before arrival, the order of eviction among idle sessions, and a learned price. The record with the numbers, settings and names of each iteration is in the repository.
