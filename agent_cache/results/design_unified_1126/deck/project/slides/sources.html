Nothing in the design needs new mathematics. Each rule is a classic result moved to this setting, and this slide lists them so that a reader can check each one. The price of a GPU-second comes from the response time law of a closed loop, from the textbook of Lazowska and co-authors. The landing point of a read after arrival is the newsvendor quantile of Arrow, Harris and Marschak: the cost of being late against the cost of holding. The idea that the application discloses a hint and that one cost model allocates memory between prefetching and caching goes back to informed prefetching by Patterson and co-authors and to the four rules of Cao and co-authors. The holding index is the Gittins index of a job whose size is not known, here the gap, in the form given by Aalto and by Scully and Harchol-Balter. With exact hints it is Belady’s rule, evict the farthest next use, and with one falling-rate law it is the result of Panigrahy and co-authors that keeping the largest hazard is best. Prices for shared room come from relaxing a capacity so that it holds on average, as in Everett and in Whittle’s restless bandits. The order on the SSD is Jackson’s earliest due date and Smith’s ratio rule, and who yields host room is the divisible knapsack. For many sessions sharing a capacity, index policies are known to approach the priced bound as the number of sessions grows, under conditions that one constraint and a stable fluid limit provide. Three shared resources and a queue do not meet those conditions, so that part of the design is a heuristic, and the deck says so.
